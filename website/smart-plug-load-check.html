Smart Plug Load Check

Enter your plug rating and the device you want to automate. You get a green, amber or red verdict, the load against the 80% safety margin, and the four questions to ask.

Read it on the plug or its box: it is printed in amps and volts.
If the label shows amps only, choose amps: watts = volts x amps.

How the check works

It applies the same rules as our guide to what you can plug into a smart plug:

  • Plug limit: volts x amps. A 15 A plug at 120 V is about 1800 W; a 13 A plug at 230 V is about 2990 W; a 10 A plug at 230 V is about 2300 W.
  • Safety margin: keep the load under about 80% of the rating, especially for devices that run for hours.
  • Red devices: heaters, kettles, irons, toasters, hair dryers, fridges, freezers and anything that must never lose power should not go on a smart plug, whatever their watts.
  • Amber devices: motors and compressors draw extra current at start-up, so they need extra care.

Typical watts are rough guides and vary a lot between models, so use the real label whenever you can. The result is general information, not electrical advice. This tool has no affiliate links.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many watts can a smart plug handle?

Multiply the volts by the amps printed on the plug. A 15 A plug on 120 V is about 1800 W, and a 10 A plug on 230 V is about 2300 W. Keep the load under about 80% of that figure.

Why is a 1500 W space heater on a 1800 W plug a bad idea?

It looks like it fits, but it is over the 80% line, it makes heat and it runs for hours. Most heater manuals say to plug the heater straight into the wall.

Can I use a smart plug with a fridge or freezer?

It is not recommended. The compressor draws extra power when it starts, and one accidental switch-off can spoil the food inside.

What if my device label only shows amps?

Choose the amps option. The calculator multiplies the amps by the voltage of your plug to get watts.
