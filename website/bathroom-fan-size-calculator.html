How the calculator works

It applies the same rules as our guide to choosing a bathroom exhaust fan:

  • Size rule: the Home Ventilating Institute (HVI) rule of thumb is 1 CFM per square foot for bathrooms up to 100 square feet. For larger bathrooms it adds 50 CFM for each toilet, shower and tub and 100 CFM for a jetted tub.
  • Minimum: ASHRAE 62.2 and the US residential code set at least 50 CFM for a fan you switch on and off, or 20 CFM for one that runs continuously. The target is the larger of the two numbers.
  • What to shop for: headline ratings are measured at a light load. ENERGY STAR advises a fan rated at least 70 CFM when you need 50; the same ratio scaled to other sizes is our estimate.
  • Duct: the minimum duct sizes come from the California Energy Commission table, rated at 0.25 in. w.g. It applies in California and is a useful yardstick elsewhere.

The results are planning figures. Local code, the fan maker's manual and the real duct run decide the installation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What CFM bathroom fan do I need?

Start with the bathroom floor area in square feet for bathrooms up to 100 sq ft, and treat 50 CFM as the minimum for a fan you switch on and off. Over 100 sq ft, add 50 CFM for each toilet, shower and tub and 100 CFM for a jetted tub. The calculator applies these rules and shows your target.

Why does the calculator suggest a higher rating to shop for?

Fan listings usually show airflow at a light load (0.1 in. w.g.), and the same fan delivers less once it is ducted. ENERGY STAR advises 70 CFM for a 50 CFM need; the calculator scales that ratio to other sizes as an estimate, so check the rating at 0.25 in. w.g. when the listing gives it.

Does it work outside the United States?

The rules come from US sources (HVI, ASHRAE 62.2, ENERGY STAR and a California duct table). Other countries set their own minimums; England, for example, uses litres per second in Approved Document F, as our guide explains. Use local rules there.

Is this a code check?

No. It is a planning tool. Vent the fan outdoors, follow the manual of the fan you buy and your local code, and ask a licensed professional about the duct run or wiring when in doubt.
