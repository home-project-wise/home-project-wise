Home Project Calculator

Measure a space, estimate materials, check an appliance's running cost or build a project estimate. Use your own measurements, product coverage and local prices—nothing is sent to a server.

Room area and volume

Calculate floor area, perimeter and—if you enter a depth or height—volume. Choose the unit you measured in.

Formulas: area = length × width; perimeter = 2 × (length + width); volume = area × depth. Unit conversions use 1 ft = 0.3048 m exactly.

Paint quantity and cost

Estimate paint from the room dimensions, number of coats, the coverage printed on your paint tin and the price you enter.

Add the combined area you will not paint.

Formula: (2 × (length + width) × wall height − openings) × coats × (1 + allowance) ÷ coverage per tin. The tin count rounds up to a whole tin. Ceilings, textured walls, primer and unusual room shapes are not included.

Flooring, tile and material packs

Use this for products sold by pack, box, roll or container when the label states how much area one unit covers.

Formula: surface area = length × width; required packs = round up[(area × (1 + allowance)) ÷ coverage per pack]. Check pack coverage, pattern matching, cuts, substrate and supplier return rules before ordering.

Electricity running cost

Estimate the energy use and running cost of one appliance from its rated wattage, daily use and your electricity tariff.

Formula: energy (kWh) = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours per day × days. Cost = kWh × your entered price per kWh. Actual use varies with cycling, settings, standby draw and your tariff structure; this is not an electrical safety check.

Project cost estimate

Add the materials and services you expect to pay for. Enter prices from your own suppliers and include extras you know apply.

Formula: materials subtotal = quantity × unit price; contingency = (materials + extras) × allowance; tax is applied to the subtotal plus contingency. Edit the inputs to match the way tax and fees are charged in your location.

Wallpaper rolls

Plan wallpaper from the exact numbers printed on the product label. Every figure uses the one unit you choose, and each room is calculated and rounded up separately.

Product label

Half-drop, offset, mural and irregular patterns are not guessed: use the manufacturer's own calculator for those. Take every figure from the label of the exact product you will buy.

Rooms

Check this wallpaper against a budget

Method: each wall needs round up(wall width ÷ usable roll width) strips. A strip is wall height + trim allowance (+ one full pattern repeat for straight match). Strips per sale unit = round down(usable length ÷ strip length); sale units per room = round up(strips ÷ strips per sale unit). Leftovers are not carried between walls or rooms, and doors and windows are not deducted. Prices are only what you type; unknown prices stay unknown and are never treated as zero. Follow the manufacturer's instructions where they differ. See the wallpaper roll guide.

How to get a more useful estimate

  • Measure the actual area and split irregular rooms into simple shapes.
  • Use coverage and yield from the exact product label, not a generic internet average.
  • Enter your local electricity tariff and current supplier prices.
  • Keep the exact quantity separate from the extra allowance; the allowance is a planning choice, not a guarantee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these results exact?

No. They are planning estimates based on the values you enter. Measurement errors, product coverage, waste, site conditions, taxes and supplier rules can change the final amount.

Does the calculator store my measurements or prices?

No. Calculations run in your browser. The page does not send the values you enter to HomeProjectWise.

Can I use it outside the United States?

Yes. The measurement and material calculators accept metric inputs, and you can enter your own electricity tariff, prices and currency symbol.

Can it tell me whether electrical work is safe or code-compliant?

No. The electricity section estimates energy consumption and cost only. It does not assess wiring, circuit capacity, appliance suitability or electrical code requirements.
