How the calculator works

It starts from a published benchmark and then applies the cautions from our guide to sizing a crawl space dehumidifier:

  • Benchmark: 70 pints (33 litres) of moisture removal per day for every 1,000 sq ft (93 m2) of crawl space floor area, the figure in the 2018 International Residential Code option for unvented crawl spaces.
  • Cold, damp or partly sealed space: real output is lower than the label because cold air holds less moisture, so the calculator advises the next size up in the manufacturer's chart.
  • Water first: standing water, an active leak or a reading above 75% is a water problem, not a sizing problem.

The result is an estimate. Some adopted versions of the code instead say the dehumidifier must be sized according to the manufacturer's specifications, and your local building department decides what applies to you. Compare any number with the manufacturer's sizing chart and confirm the result with a 7-day humidity log. This tool has no affiliate links.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pints per day does my crawl space need?

As a starting benchmark, about 70 pints per day for every 1,000 square feet of floor area. Cold, damp or partly sealed spaces need more than the label suggests, so check the manufacturer's sizing chart and go up a size if the space is cold or very damp.

Why does the calculator say to go up a size?

A dehumidifier removes less water from cool air than its warm-conditions rating suggests. If your crawl space stays cold, is very damp or is not sealed, the next size up in the manufacturer's chart gives you a margin.

What humidity should a crawl space be?

Aim for roughly 45 to 55 percent and keep it below 60 percent. Above that, mold grows much more easily.

Is this result a code requirement?

No. It is a planning estimate. Your local building department and the code edition it has adopted decide what applies to your home.
