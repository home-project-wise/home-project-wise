Our Working Charter

The shared operating standard for every article, calculator, design decision and technical improvement. We decide what deserves to be built, learn from successful publishers without copying them, and measure whether the result helps homeowners.

Review draft — not yet approved. This page explains our public editorial and research principles. Internal candidate lists and operational backlogs are maintained separately; no topic is approved for publication by appearing in a research queue.

1. Our mission

Help homeowners make better project decisions: diagnose a problem, choose a safe next step, calculate quantities and costs, compare materials or quotes, and know when professional help is appropriate.

What we learn — and improve

  • Strong titles that promise a clear reader outcome.
  • Topic clusters and logical paths between related questions.
  • Quick answers, tools/materials, cost drivers and decision criteria.
  • Original diagrams and images that explain, not decorate.
  • Visible sourcing, real testing disclosures and correction practices.
  • Useful calculators and comparison methods connected to articles.

Rule: domain traffic is not page traffic. Every priority topic needs URL-level competitor research in the target country. Never copy text, images or unsupported claims.

4. How we select useful topics

HomeProjectWise maintains a working portfolio of possible articles and tools. It is a research queue, not a public publishing promise or a target number of pages. Candidate titles are not displayed here because they can change after checking the current site, live search results, reader needs and competing pages.

  • Start with a specific homeowner problem and one primary search intent.
  • Check whether an existing guide or calculator already solves the problem; improve that page when it is the better answer.
  • Study the actual pages ranking for the target query in the United States before approving a new brief.
  • Identify a useful advantage that competitors do not already provide, such as a tested decision path, transparent calculation, practical comparison or clear safety boundary.
  • Keep estimates, assumptions and unknowns visible. Do not invent prices, test results, credentials or demand.

5. Current opportunity themes

Our working research is prioritizing connected homeowner journeys rather than a quota of articles: home-energy checks and air-leak diagnosis; safe repair troubleshooting; smart-home compatibility where it adds a genuinely new decision method; project quantity and waste calculations; and transparent contractor-quote comparisons. Each theme remains subject to live SERP review, duplicate checks, source validation and the Value Before Publishing gate.

The detailed candidate portfolio, overlap flags, source notes and technical backlog are maintained in project working files in the repository. They are not publication approvals.

Complete existing tools first

  • Wallpaper Calculator: comparison, adapter coverage, test cases, mobile accessibility and verified Budget Planner handoff.
  • Audit the main project calculator and Budget Planner for formula transparency, validation, edge cases, accessibility and mobile behavior.
  • Connect paint, flooring, wallpaper and project-cost journeys without duplicating calculations.

Proposed new tools

  1. Tile Quantity & Cost Calculator — dimensions, layout/waste, box coverage and user-entered price.
  2. Home Energy Cost Calculator — appliance wattage, use hours and user-entered electricity rate.
  3. Insulation Estimator — area, existing insulation, target specification and package coverage.
  4. Bathroom Ventilation Calculator — room dimensions, airflow method and duct caveats.
  5. Contractor Quote Comparison Tool — scope, quantities, allowances, exclusions and unknowns.
  6. Home Repair Cost Planner — user-entered line items and transparent contingency.
  7. HVAC Filter Replacement Planner — filter details and manufacturer-guided replacement interval.

Every tool needs a defined user decision, formula/source, assumptions, test vectors, edge cases, accessibility/privacy review and a supporting guide. Never invent live prices or supplier availability.

7. Design and experience improvements

  1. Task-first article template with quick answer, suitability, tools/materials, steps, checks, troubleshooting, safety and sources as relevant.
  2. Consistent calculator inputs, units, explainable results, assumptions, reset/edit and next action.
  3. Contextual links between articles and relevant calculators.
  4. Guides discovery by problem, room, project and skill level where supported.
  5. Search that understands problem phrasing and synonyms.
  6. Useful topic hubs, breadcrumbs and related next steps.
  7. Clear source, editorial, update and correction information; no invented expert review.
  8. Mobile-first tables, forms, diagrams and long results.
  9. Original task-specific visuals rather than decorative stock imagery.
  10. Accessible focus, labels, error messages, contrast, keyboard behavior and touch targets.
  11. Responsive image loading, meaningful alt text and verification that images actually render.
  12. Visual review across representative articles, tools, hubs and search pages in mobile/desktop and light/dark modes.

8. Rules every worker must follow

  1. Strategy and written brief before production.
  2. Evidence before topic selection; label estimates and unknowns.
  3. Learn from competitors; never clone them.
  4. One primary intent per URL; check duplicates and cannibalization.
  5. Original reader value is mandatory.
  6. Quality and safety outrank volume.
  7. No invented expertise, tests, prices, statistics or experience.
  8. Use primary/authoritative sources for technical and safety claims.
  9. Preserve URLs and generated outputs; use source files/generators.
  10. Mobile-first accessibility and real screenshot inspection.
  11. Passing CI is not proof of rankings or complete visual quality.
  12. Record exact files, evidence, checks, limitations and next task in the operations room.

9. Mandatory article approval gate

  • Audience, country, problem and one primary intent.
  • Search demand evidence and query variations.
  • Current-site duplicate/cannibalization check.
  • Top-results review and 3–5 competitor page analysis.
  • Competitor strengths, verified weaknesses and our unique advantage.
  • Outline with a direct answer near the top.
  • Sources, safety boundaries and expert-review requirements.
  • Original visual/tool/checklist plan and internal links.
  • Success metric and post-release review condition.

10. Prioritization and measurement

Score reader pain, demand, pillar fit, competitive gap, original value, leverage from existing tools and feasibility. Subtract risk for duplication, weak sources, safety, uncertain formulas and maintenance. Search volume alone is never sufficient. Track Search Console/Bing clicks, impressions, CTR, position, country, device, query-to-URL fit, calculator use, indexing, broken links, accessibility, performance and visual QA.

11. Roles and approval flow

Owner: approves scope, portfolio and merges. Claude: audits inventory/SERPs, flags duplicates, reviews feasibility, implements approved work and records checks. ChatGPT: researches, independently audits and reports only visual evidence it actually accessed. All workers: read the charter, project state, inventory, work log and open PRs before starting; avoid collisions and leave a precise handoff.

Approval flow: research → brief → owner approval → implementation → editorial/technical review → visual QA → PR checks → owner merge decision → post-release measurement.

No publication dates are assigned. Work is selected by approved priority and readiness, not calendar pressure. The 100 titles and tool/design ideas remain proposals until owner and Claude review them.

The full operating charter and evidence checklist are maintained in the repository. The public Editorial Policy explains our research and corrections approach.

12. Mandatory weekly research and continuous improvement

Living charter: the initial competitor scan and 100-title portfolio are a starting hypothesis, not a permanent market map. ChatGPT must perform a fresh research and review cycle at least once every seven days and use verified findings to improve this charter and its candidate portfolio.

Weekly research duties

  1. Recheck target-country search results for priority topics; record exact queries, country, date, ranking URLs and meaningful SERP changes.
  2. Revisit relevant major publishers, specialists and authoritative sources. Find useful new pages, tools, formats, clusters and material changes.
  3. Keep domain traffic estimates, keyword search volume, estimated page traffic and measured site traffic separate. Label dates, geography, provider and limitations.
  4. Identify new reader questions, terminology, product/platform changes, seasonal needs, safety guidance and underserved topics.
  5. Recheck the live sitemap, content inventory, tools, open PRs and available Search Console/Bing data. Never rely on stale counts or assume a topic remains uncovered.
  6. Reassess article candidates and tool/design priorities. Keep, improve, reorder, replace or retire ideas based on evidence, usefulness, feasibility and duplication risk. One hundred is not a quota.
  7. Find weaknesses in our own pages, tools and user journeys, then recommend specific improvements—not superficial competitor imitation.

Evidence and weekly handoff

Every review must leave a dated, concise research note in the operations room, with source URLs, queries/country, competitor page URLs, what changed, verified facts versus estimates, implications for this site, exact proposed charter edits, priority/confidence, risks and decisions for the owner. Unavailable or unverified evidence must be labelled; never invent traffic, rankings, trends or competitor weaknesses.

Update authority and protected decisions

ChatGPT maintains the evidence-informed proposal: refresh research, refine candidates, priorities and gap analysis, and keep this page consistent with the main charter and change history. Mission/scope, new pillars, publication or URL decisions, redirects/deletions, major architecture/design changes, tool releases, publishing, merging and spending remain owner-approved decisions. Weekly research does not authorize implementation or publication. Claude reviews material feasibility changes and flags conflicts. Check open PRs before proposing overlapping work. If repository or analytics access is unavailable, report the limitation and provide a proposed patch; never claim an update was made.

Weekly decision loop: Observe → verify → compare → identify gaps → revise proposal → record changes → request approval → measure later. Keep strong decisions when evidence still supports them; avoid change for its own sake, speculative edits, publication deadlines and output quotas.

This weekly review law is part of the consolidated proposal and takes effect as an operating requirement once approved by the owner.

13. Competitive intelligence system and mandatory competitor dossiers

Purpose: replace a static competitor-name list with a repeatable, evidence-led system showing which sites and pages win target searches, why they win, where they are weak, and how HomeProjectWise can provide a more useful answer. The seven publishers in the initial benchmark are a starting cohort only; specialist sites must also be discovered from live target-country search results.

Competitor universe: three layers

  1. Major publisher cohort: Good Housekeeping, The Spruce, Martha Stewart, HGTV, Real Simple, Houzz and Better Homes & Gardens.
  2. Topic-level specialist competitors: smaller focused sites that repeatedly rank for a particular problem, room, repair, product, calculator or project. Discover them from actual SERPs; do not exclude them because their domain traffic is small.
  3. Reference and authority sources: manufacturers, standards bodies, utilities, government agencies, trade associations and qualified professionals. These inform accuracy and safety even when they do not compete for broad traffic.

A domain is not automatically a competitor for every topic. Assign relevance by pillar, query cluster and page intent. Record the reason for inclusion and date last validated. Add new competitors and retire irrelevant ones with a dated reason; do not freeze the list at seven.

Required individual competitor dossier

Create and maintain one dossier per relevant competitor. Use Not publicly available, Not verified or Not applicable rather than guessing.

  1. Identity and scope: domain, site type, target countries/languages, relevant pillars, audience, business model where observable and reason for inclusion.
  2. Content inventory: best available count of published editorial articles, method, source, date, scope and confidence. Prefer reproducible sitemap/CMS/category/archive inventory, deduplicated by canonical URL and filtered to genuine editorial articles. Separate guides, news, recipes, reviews, videos, community pages, profiles, category/tag pages, tools and other indexable URLs. If exact counting is impossible, report a range and method. Indexed URLs and SEO-tool URL counts are not article counts.
  3. Top 20 performing pages: up to 20 relevant URLs with title, canonical URL, topic/pillar, format, target country, ranking queries, position/date where available, estimated organic visits/provider/date, and measured traffic only if first-party analytics were legitimately disclosed. State the ranking metric. If page traffic is unavailable, use the best transparent proxy and label it.
  4. Top 10 topic clusters: strongest demonstrated search-presence clusters in the target market, with representative queries, intent, ranking URLs, observed top-10 coverage across a fixed query sample, country and observation date. Explain the sample. Do not claim topic ownership from one query.
  5. Traffic-driving formats: classify winning pages as how-to, troubleshooting, checklist, comparison, buying guide, review, calculator, template, gallery, video, FAQ, inspiration or another observed format; explain which intents they serve.
  6. Tools and interactive assets: calculators, quizzes, selectors, planners, templates and other functional assets. Record URL, purpose, inputs/outputs, assumptions, mobile usability, accessibility, monetization and verifiability. Separate interactive tools from static articles and promotional widgets.
  7. Editorial strengths: demonstrated expertise, author/reviewer transparency, sourcing, firsthand testing, specificity, clarity, original examples, visuals, updates, safety and corrections. Cite examples; do not infer quality from reputation alone.
  8. Technical and UX strengths: navigation, information architecture, internal links, measured performance, mobile layout, accessibility, structured data, images, search/filtering, ad load and intrusive elements. Record method, device, date and tool.
  9. Commercial strengths: affiliate disclosures, product testing, retailer links, lead generation, directories, subscriptions, sponsorships, commerce and advertising. Record observable evidence and distinguish it from inference.
  10. Weaknesses and exploitable gaps: reproducible shortcomings such as missing steps, unclear assumptions, outdated information, weak local relevance, inaccessible controls, poor mobile presentation, thin comparisons, missing calculators or unanswered questions. Cite URL/test/screenshot, date and impact. Never invent weaknesses.
  11. Our better-answer opportunity: specific improvement for a defined reader and intent, including missing value, required evidence/expertise, original visual/tool/checklist, safety boundary, maintenance cost and why it is meaningfully better—not merely longer.
  12. Confidence and follow-up: evidence quality, unknowns, last checked date, next verification, owner/Claude review needs and change history.

Article-count methodology

  • High confidence: complete accessible sitemap or content export with canonical URLs, cross-checked against archives and a sample of live URLs.
  • Medium confidence: systematic archive/category pagination or crawl with deduplication, coverage gaps and sampling check.
  • Low confidence: third-party SEO index or search-engine index estimate only; label it an indexed-page proxy, never an article count.

For every count, state retrieval date, domain/subdomain scope, included/excluded types, duplicate handling, crawl/pagination coverage and confidence. Give a range if incomplete. Do not compare totals unless inclusion rules and collection methods are comparable. Track major formats separately when useful and reconcile later counts rather than silently overwriting them.

Page-level research and ranking evidence

  1. Define target country, language, intent and fixed query set before reviewing results.
  2. Capture the first results page (normally top 10; extend to top 20 when justified), including relevant SERP features. Record date, query, country, device and ranking URL.
  3. Inspect actual pages—not only homepages, domain scores or snippets—including content, visuals, tools, citations, update dates and user journey.
  4. Where available, collect page-level estimated organic traffic, ranking keywords, positions and trends from an SEO platform. Record provider, database/country, date and metric definitions. These remain estimates.
  5. Use first-party traffic only for HomeProjectWise when authorized Search Console/analytics access is actually available. Competitor measured analytics are not public unless credibly disclosed.
  6. Save direct URLs and evidence notes. Snippets and cached extracts alone cannot establish page quality, functionality or weakness.

Specialist competitor discovery

  • At each weekly review and each new content cluster, search target-country SERPs for primary and supporting queries.
  • Record recurring domains across multiple relevant queries, even if small, focused, local or expert-led.
  • Check specialist repair, DIY, organization, energy, smart-home, building-science and trade sources when relevant.
  • Separate commercial competitors from technical authorities and manufacturer documentation.
  • Add a specialist to the active dossier set when repeated relevant visibility or unique expertise is demonstrated; record the exact queries and ranking URLs.
  • Topic-level SERP evidence outranks overall domain size when deciding who competes for a specific topic.

Comparative scorecard and decision rules

Score each relevant competitor/page cluster from 1–5, attaching evidence and confidence to every score:

  • Search visibility and page-level performance evidence.
  • Intent match and answer completeness.
  • Accuracy, sourcing, expertise and safety.
  • Originality and practical usefulness.
  • Visual explanation and interactive utility.
  • Mobile UX, accessibility and technical quality.
  • Internal discovery and related-topic coverage.
  • Trust, transparency and update discipline.

A score without a cited observation is unscored, not zero. Do not collapse dimensions into one league table without documented weights and purpose. For each HomeProjectWise opportunity, state what competitors do well, what is missing or weak, our specific credible improvement, required original evidence/assets/expert review, production and maintenance cost, and cannibalization risk.

Deliverables, ownership and cadence

Maintain a competitor register linking to dossiers, dated topic-level SERP studies and a concise operations-room comparison. All seven initial major publishers need a dossier; add specialist dossiers as evidence warrants. Do not fabricate a top-20 list when the data cannot support it—record the limitation and best defensible proxy.

  • Weekly: discover relevant specialist domains, refresh priority query samples and update decision-relevant dossiers.
  • For every new article/tool proposal: study 3–5 actual ranking pages (or all relevant results if fewer exist) before approval.
  • Periodic deep review: refresh all seven major-publisher dossiers in a planned rotation; never claim all are freshly audited unless each was actually reviewed.
  • ChatGPT: independent research, source checking, gap analysis and proposal updates; disclose access limits.
  • Claude: validate inventory and feasibility, inspect URLs/tools, challenge unsupported weakness claims and check active-work overlap.
  • Owner: approves strategy, scope, production, publishing and merges.

Evidence integrity and prohibited shortcuts

Never invent article counts, page traffic, rankings, keyword ownership, conversions, tool functionality, credentials or weaknesses. Never present domain-wide traffic as article traffic, search volume as page visits, indexed URLs as articles, or SEO estimates as measured analytics. Respect source terms, robots.txt, rate limits, licensing and privacy. If a platform/paywall/account is unavailable, state the limitation and continue with accessible primary evidence.

Acceptance gate: a dossier is decision-ready only when scope, date, method, sources, article-count confidence, page-level evidence, topic sample, tools/formats, strengths, evidenced weaknesses, HomeProjectWise opportunity and unknowns are recorded. A competitor list without these dossiers is not a completed competitive analysis.
