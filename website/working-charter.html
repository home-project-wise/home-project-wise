HOW WE WORK · 2026–2027

Our Working Charter

A shared operating standard for every article, calculator, design decision and technical improvement. We choose the work before we start it, learn from successful publishers without copying them, and measure whether our work actually helps homeowners.

Version 0.1 · Foundation draft · 10 November 2026 – 9 November 2027

Our promise: HomeProjectWise will not publish just to keep busy. Every project must solve a defined reader problem, have evidence behind it, add something original and pass clear quality checks.

1. Our mission

Help homeowners make better project decisions: understand the problem, choose a safe next step, calculate what is needed, estimate their own costs and know when professional help is appropriate.

What we copy as a lesson — never as content

  • Strong, descriptive titles that promise a useful outcome.
  • Topic clusters and clear paths between related problems.
  • Visible quick answers, project scope, tools/materials and decision criteria.
  • Useful diagrams, project photos and checklists that explain rather than decorate.
  • Editorial transparency, source quality, real testing disclosures and clear corrections.
  • Comparison criteria, cost drivers and tools that help the reader take the next step.

Important distinction: a large site's traffic does not prove that every page or topic is strong. Each target URL must be researched separately. For example, third-party keyword snapshots show Real Simple ranking for “how to organize closet” and Apartment Therapy ranking for “how to organize desk drawers”; those topics are not automatically in scope for HomeProjectWise because our separate Home Organization Ideas project owns generic organization content. We study their title and intent framing, then apply the lesson only to our own mission.

4. Rules every worker must follow

  1. Strategy before production: no article, calculator or redesign without a written brief linked to an annual objective.
  2. Evidence before selection: check Search Console, live SERPs, user questions and competitor pages; label estimates clearly.
  3. Learn, do not clone: never copy wording, outlines, images, data or unsupported claims.
  4. Reader outcome first: each deliverable must help a reader understand, decide, calculate, compare, complete or safely stop.
  5. Original value is mandatory: add a transparent calculation, diagram, decision tree, worked example, checklist or other real advantage.
  6. Quality beats volume: no daily article quota; weak work is not published to fill a calendar.
  7. One primary intent per URL: check overlap before creating a new page; improve an existing page when appropriate.
  8. Safety and honesty: no invented tests, expertise, prices, statistics or experience; use primary sources for technical claims.
  9. Mobile-first: test phone, tablet and desktop; check accessibility, keyboard behavior and dark mode where supported.
  10. Preserve live assets: no URL, formula, image or navigation changes without impact review, tests and a PR.
  11. Measure after release: a passing build is not proof of rankings or user value.
  12. Handoff: record exact files, checks, limitations and the next task in the operations room.

5. The mandatory brief before any article

  • Audience, country, problem and one primary search intent.
  • Primary query, related questions and title alternatives derived from actual SERPs.
  • Existing URL overlap and cannibalization check.
  • Search Console evidence or a documented reason to enter a new topic.
  • At least five live SERP results and three competitor pages reviewed.
  • What competitors do well, what is missing, and our specific original advantage.
  • Outline with a direct answer near the top, sources, safety limits and expert-review needs.
  • Original visual/tool plan, internal links, success metric and review date.

6. Calculator and product rules

We build calculators to solve meaningful decisions, not to increase the tool count. Every calculator needs documented inputs and units, formula and source, assumptions, rounding, test cases, edge cases, privacy behavior, mobile/accessibility checks and limitations. The strategic direction is to connect measurement → quantity and waste → pack/unit conversion → user-entered price → quote comparison → project budget. Never invent live prices or supplier availability.

7. Design and technical rules

  • Clear next action, consistent navigation, topic hubs, breadcrumbs, search and contextual related links.
  • Readable type, sufficient contrast, visible keyboard focus, accessible labels, alt text and touch targets.
  • Task-specific visuals that explain placement, measurements, compatibility or outcomes; no decorative image quotas.
  • Fast, responsive pages with working internal links, valid metadata, canonical URLs and structured data.
  • Automated browser QA on mobile/tablet/desktop and light/dark; screenshots must be inspected before declaring visual success.

9. How we decide what gets done first

Each proposal is scored for reader pain, demand evidence, mission fit, competitive gap, original value, internal/tool leverage and delivery confidence. We subtract risk for duplication, weak sources, unsafe complexity, uncertain formulas and heavy maintenance. No proposal begins without a distinct reason to exist and a documented owner priority.

10. How we measure success

  • Monthly: Search Console and Bing clicks, impressions, CTR, position, country, device and query-to-URL fit.
  • Monthly: useful analytics such as engaged visits, calculator starts/completions and next-step clicks.
  • Monthly: indexing, broken links, structured data, performance, accessibility and visual QA.
  • Quarterly: competitor review and backlog reprioritization.
  • Every six months: substantive fact/source review of evergreen pages.
  • Annually: compare with the November 2026 baseline and approve the next plan.

11. What success means after one year

Not a target number of articles. Success means more qualified organic clicks than the baseline, more pages earning meaningful impressions, useful calculator completion, stronger topic clusters, fewer overlapping/orphan pages, better mobile and accessibility quality, trustworthy sources and a distinctive workflow from problem diagnosis to project budget.
