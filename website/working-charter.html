HOW WE WORK · OWNER AND CLAUDE REVIEW

Our Working Charter

The shared operating standard for every article, calculator, design decision and technical improvement. We decide what deserves to be built, learn from successful publishers without copying them, and measure whether the result helps homeowners.

Review draft — not yet approved. This charter contains no publication dates or monthly quotas. The 100 titles below are candidate ideas, not authorization to publish. Each must pass competitor research, duplicate checks and owner approval.

1. Our mission

Help homeowners make better project decisions: diagnose a problem, choose a safe next step, calculate quantities and costs, compare materials or quotes, and know when professional help is appropriate.

What we learn — and improve

  • Strong titles that promise a clear reader outcome.
  • Topic clusters and logical paths between related questions.
  • Quick answers, tools/materials, cost drivers and decision criteria.
  • Original diagrams and images that explain, not decorate.
  • Visible sourcing, real testing disclosures and correction practices.
  • Useful calculators and comparison methods connected to articles.

Rule: domain traffic is not page traffic. Every priority topic needs URL-level competitor research in the target country. Never copy text, images or unsupported claims.

6. Calculator and tool roadmap

Complete existing tools first

  • Wallpaper Calculator: comparison, adapter coverage, test cases, mobile accessibility and verified Budget Planner handoff.
  • Audit the main project calculator and Budget Planner for formula transparency, validation, edge cases, accessibility and mobile behavior.
  • Connect paint, flooring, wallpaper and project-cost journeys without duplicating calculations.

Proposed new tools

  1. Tile Quantity & Cost Calculator — dimensions, layout/waste, box coverage and user-entered price.
  2. Home Energy Cost Calculator — appliance wattage, use hours and user-entered electricity rate.
  3. Insulation Estimator — area, existing insulation, target specification and package coverage.
  4. Bathroom Ventilation Calculator — room dimensions, airflow method and duct caveats.
  5. Contractor Quote Comparison Tool — scope, quantities, allowances, exclusions and unknowns.
  6. Home Repair Cost Planner — user-entered line items and transparent contingency.
  7. HVAC Filter Replacement Planner — filter details and manufacturer-guided replacement interval.

Every tool needs a defined user decision, formula/source, assumptions, test vectors, edge cases, accessibility/privacy review and a supporting guide. Never invent live prices or supplier availability.

7. Design and experience improvements

  1. Task-first article template with quick answer, suitability, tools/materials, steps, checks, troubleshooting, safety and sources as relevant.
  2. Consistent calculator inputs, units, explainable results, assumptions, reset/edit and next action.
  3. Contextual links between articles and relevant calculators.
  4. Guides discovery by problem, room, project and skill level where supported.
  5. Search that understands problem phrasing and synonyms.
  6. Useful topic hubs, breadcrumbs and related next steps.
  7. Clear source, editorial, update and correction information; no invented expert review.
  8. Mobile-first tables, forms, diagrams and long results.
  9. Original task-specific visuals rather than decorative stock imagery.
  10. Accessible focus, labels, error messages, contrast, keyboard behavior and touch targets.
  11. Responsive image loading, meaningful alt text and verification that images actually render.
  12. Visual review across representative articles, tools, hubs and search pages in mobile/desktop and light/dark modes.

8. Rules every worker must follow

  1. Strategy and written brief before production.
  2. Evidence before topic selection; label estimates and unknowns.
  3. Learn from competitors; never clone them.
  4. One primary intent per URL; check duplicates and cannibalization.
  5. Original reader value is mandatory.
  6. Quality and safety outrank volume.
  7. No invented expertise, tests, prices, statistics or experience.
  8. Use primary/authoritative sources for technical and safety claims.
  9. Preserve URLs and generated outputs; use source files/generators.
  10. Mobile-first accessibility and real screenshot inspection.
  11. Passing CI is not proof of rankings or complete visual quality.
  12. Record exact files, evidence, checks, limitations and next task in the operations room.

9. Mandatory article approval gate

  • Audience, country, problem and one primary intent.
  • Search demand evidence and query variations.
  • Current-site duplicate/cannibalization check.
  • Top-results review and 3–5 competitor page analysis.
  • Competitor strengths, verified weaknesses and our unique advantage.
  • Outline with a direct answer near the top.
  • Sources, safety boundaries and expert-review requirements.
  • Original visual/tool/checklist plan and internal links.
  • Success metric and post-release review condition.

10. Prioritization and measurement

Score reader pain, demand, pillar fit, competitive gap, original value, leverage from existing tools and feasibility. Subtract risk for duplication, weak sources, safety, uncertain formulas and maintenance. Search volume alone is never sufficient. Track Search Console/Bing clicks, impressions, CTR, position, country, device, query-to-URL fit, calculator use, indexing, broken links, accessibility, performance and visual QA.

11. Roles and approval flow

Owner: approves scope, portfolio and merges. Claude: audits inventory/SERPs, flags duplicates, reviews feasibility, implements approved work and records checks. ChatGPT: researches, independently audits and reports only visual evidence it actually accessed. All workers: read the charter, project state, inventory, work log and open PRs before starting; avoid collisions and leave a precise handoff.

Approval flow: research → brief → owner approval → implementation → editorial/technical review → visual QA → PR checks → owner merge decision → post-release measurement.

No publication dates are assigned. Work is selected by approved priority and readiness, not calendar pressure. The 100 titles and tool/design ideas remain proposals until owner and Claude review them.

The full operating charter and evidence checklist are maintained in the repository. The public Editorial Policy explains our research and corrections approach.
