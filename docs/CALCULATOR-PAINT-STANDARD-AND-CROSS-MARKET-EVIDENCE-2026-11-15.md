# Paint spreading-rate standard and cross-market evidence — 2026-11-15

**Owner:** ChatGPT — research, evidence quality and assumptions  
**Purpose:** Determine a defensible method for a beginner paint estimator, and expand comparable product evidence across the eight currently supported markets.  
**Status:** Methodological evidence review; no generic assumption is approved in this document.

## Executive finding

A reliable solution is possible, but **not by averaging every manufacturer's headline “coverage” figure**. The word *coverage* is ambiguous: it can mean hiding power/opacity or spreading rate. A technically defensible estimator must compare the same measure, product class, application basis and substrate scenario.

The strongest methodological source located is **ISO 6504-3:2019**, confirmed current by ISO in 2025. It specifies hiding-power methods for white/light-coloured masonry, concrete and interior paints, and a simplified spreading-rate method for low-volatility coatings such as interior wall/ceiling coatings. The standard explicitly distinguishes:
- spreading rate: area covered by a quantity of coating to achieve a required dried film thickness;
- practical spreading rate: what is obtained on the particular substrate in practice;
- theoretical spreading rate: calculated solely from non-volatile volume;
- hiding power: ability to obscure substrate colour differences.

Therefore, a label such as H10/contrast class 1 is not a standalone practical m²/L promise, and theoretical yield is not interchangeable with practical roller yield.

## Standards and comparability rules

### ISO 6504-3:2019 — current international test method

Official ISO page: https://www.iso.org/standard/70903.html

- Edition 3, published November 2019; ISO page says reviewed and confirmed in 2025, so it remains current.
- Scope: hiding power of white/light-coloured paints for masonry, concrete and interior use, applied to black/white charts or transparent foil over black/white panels.
- Also includes a simplified spreading-rate calculation for low-volatile paints, including interior wall and ceiling coatings as specified in EN 13300.
- The standard's definitions distinguish theoretical from practical spreading rate and define hiding power separately.
- The term “coverage” is ambiguous; technical comparisons should retain the exact term and test basis.

Accessible standard text/definitions: https://www.iteh.eu/catalog/standards/iso/c6b4b183-3508-438c-846e-3d549c6bfe31/iso-6504-3-2019

### EN 13300:2023 — European interior wall/ceiling coating classification

Accessible sample: https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/68667/37348b36d8e244c2ac96d4f4ad19622f/SIST-EN-13300-2023.pdf

- H10 hiding-power classes are contrast-ratio classes: class 1 ≥99.5%; class 2 98% to less than 99.5%; class 3 95% to less than 98%; class 4 below 95%.
- The class is tied to the spreading rate at which it is measured. Brillux explains this explicitly and gives “H10 class 1 at 7 m²/L” as an example: https://www.brillux.de/anwendungen/innenbereich/innenfarben-fuer-jede-anforderung/norm-en-13300/
- A high hiding class does not automatically establish the same number of coats on every substrate, nor does it define the practical spreading rate on a homeowner's rough/porous wall.

### ASTM D2805 — US hiding-power test

Accessible preview: https://avadocuments2026.com/preview/astm-d2805-11-2023.pdf

- Instrumental hiding-power test; defines spreading rate in m²/L and hiding power at a specified contrast ratio.
- It is a laboratory hiding-power method, not a universal real-wall practical yield.
- Do not assume EN 13300 H10 values and US ASTM values are directly interchangeable without checking the product's test basis.

## What can be concluded now

1. **A reliable estimator can be built from a declared scenario, not a pretend universal fact.** The estimator should calculate wall area × visitor-selected coat count ÷ a market- and scenario-matched spreading-rate range.
2. The default rate must come from a matched evidence set: interior wall matt/emulsion, roller application, normal prepared substrate, with the same per-coat basis and comparable hiding/contrast performance. The data must not mix:
   - theoretical and practical yields;
   - per-coat rates and total-system rates;
   - standard matt emulsion and trade/high-opacity/decorative/specialist finishes;
   - smooth sound surfaces and rough/porous/new plaster;
   - wall paints and lacquers/trim paints;
   - values for spray with values for roller.
3. **Coats are a separate variable.** Manufacturer evidence shows one-coat claims have conditions, some products specify two coats, and new/porous surfaces may need primer plus finish coats. The beginner flow should ask the visitor for coat count or surface/project condition, not silently infer one universal count. Advanced product-specific instructions can override.
4. Do not add an arbitrary 10% waste/allowance to litres. Paint application loss, substrate absorption and surface texture are not interchangeable with a generic cutting-waste percentage. If a practical range already includes the specified application conditions, do not add a second hidden factor.
5. Where a country sample is not yet comparable, the honest result is a quantity interval labelled “indicative, standard prepared surface” with explicit limitations, or no automatic litres estimate yet. A price estimate remains unavailable unless the relevant product/cost basis is separately supported.

## Proposed release gate for a beginner paint default

A country/scenario default may be proposed only when all of these are true:

- At least three independent manufacturers in that country have current, opened TDS/product evidence for ordinary interior matt wall paint (not three SKUs from one brand).
- At least two sources explicitly state per-coat spread rate or enough data to derive it without mixing total-system figures; practical roller yield is preferred.
- Each record has exact finish, substrate, application method, yield basis, film thickness/hiding class where available, coats/primer guidance, pack format, source URL, revision date and retrieval date.
- Values are normalized to m²/L per coat, but original units and basis are preserved.
- Exclude special products and non-comparable finish classes, and document exclusions.
- Derive an interval from the comparable sample (not median/midpoint unless a later approved methodology explicitly requires it); do not use the most optimistic “up to” value as the low-consumption bound.
- Test the interval against at least one manufacturer calculator/TDS example and a sample project, and make clear that it is not a quote or guaranteed purchase quantity.
- If these criteria fail in one market, do not borrow another country's rate or currency.
