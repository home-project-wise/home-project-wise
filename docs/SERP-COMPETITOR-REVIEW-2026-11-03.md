# HomeProjectWise — Query-level SERP competitor review

**Prepared:** 2026-11-03  
**Scope:** First-pass page-level comparison for the search intents already visible in Google Search Console. This is not a claim that HomeProjectWise beats the top 20 sites. Search-result samples can vary by location, device and time; each competitor example is tied to a specific query/task.

## Executive conclusion

The strongest opportunity is not to copy large sites' breadth. HomeProjectWise already has several useful differentiators on the pages that are earning impressions: visible formulas/methods, practical checklists, safety limits, source links and a direct answer to the exact query. However, there are still gaps in local context, original tools, first-hand evidence, and the precision of some cost claims.

**No competitor should be described as weak based on a brand-wide assumption.** Compare the exact page that competes for the same task. Do not award a numerical “we beat them” score until the same rubric has been applied to the complete competing page and our live page.

## 1. Basement toilet without breaking concrete

**Observed HPW signal:** the exact query “basement bathroom without breaking concrete” appeared once at average position 23; the page received 109 impressions at average position 11.28 for 2026-10-06–2026-10-31.

### Competing page evidence

| Competitor page | Demonstrated strength | Context / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| EasyBathrooms — “Basement Bathroom Without a Rough-In: Ejector Pump, Upflush Toilet or Breaking Concrete” | Exact query match; compares three routes; discusses noise, maintenance, power outages, permits, and the full installed scope | Local Newmarket, Ontario service context; it correctly avoids pretending one installed price applies everywhere |
| BuildNP — “How to Install Bathroom in Basement” | Broad project sequence, permits, layout, plumbing, electrical and finish stages; explains gravity vs ejector vs upflush | Contractor perspective and local service context; some cost/code statements require local verification |
| Overalls and Lab Coats — “How to Install a Saniflo Macerating Toilet in a Basement” | First-person project narrative, step-by-step install overview, tools/materials, practical experience | Their first-hand experience is their own; HomeProjectWise must not imitate or imply that it installed/tested a unit |
| Pumpendium — “Macerating Upflush Toilets: How They Work and What They Can Drain” | Short direct answer, technical pipe/lift overview, trade-offs and maintenance | General guide; model limits still need checking against the selected manufacturer's manual |

### What HomeProjectWise already does well

The current HPW guide already has several substantial strengths:
- A three-way comparison of gravity drainage, a macerating toilet and a conventional sewage ejector.
- A decision order that checks gravity drainage first, then compares pumped options.
- Model-specific lift/run examples with the explicit warning that maximums cannot necessarily be achieved together.
- Manufacturer documentation and a model plumbing-code reference near the claims.
- Clear trade-offs: power, noise, service access, venting, wipes, model-specific fixtures and permits.
- A transparent affiliate disclosure and no claim that the editorial team has tested the products.

This is stronger than a generic “upflush is cheaper” sales page on technical transparency and source traceability.

### Where the competing pages expose an opportunity

The recent EasyBathrooms and BuildNP pages treat the **whole basement bathroom project**, not just the toilet mechanism. The visitor needs to account for:
- existing rough-in and drain elevation;
- slab cutting/trenching and floor restoration;
- ejector basin/pump or macerator equipment;
- plumbing, venting and electrical;
- permits, inspections and local code;
- fixture count (half bath vs full bath);
- maintenance, noise, service access and power outage behaviour.

HPW mentions many of these but could make them easier to compare in one pre-quote worksheet. That would be a distinct practical benefit, not another generic article.

### Critical factual check before any edit

The current HPW guide gives rough planning ranges for equipment ($500–$1,500), plumbing installation ($500–$2,000) and electrician work ($100–$500), but its visible source list does not identify a source, date or geography for those ranges. This is not sufficiently traceable under the Value Before Publishing rule. After the 2026-11-05 checkpoint, either:
1. add a reliable source with market, date and scope for each range; or
2. remove the unsourced ranges and replace them with a transparent user-entered quote worksheet (equipment, plumbing, electrical, venting, permits, slab work/restoration and finishing).

Do not invent national averages or copy local competitor prices as universal facts.

### Proposed post-checkpoint improvement

- Test a more query-aligned H1/title such as “Basement Toilet Without Breaking Concrete: Is a Macerating System Right?” only if the live SERP and Search Console comparison support it.
- Add a short decision table for half-bath vs full-bath, fixture count, slab work, power and maintenance only where the manufacturer/code sources support each row.
- Add a project-scope worksheet rather than a made-up installed-price figure.
- Keep the existing manufacturer specifications, source list, affiliate disclosure and professional/code warnings intact.

## 2. Bathroom condensation and dampness

**Observed HPW signal:** 17 visible query variants, 36 impressions; page-level average position 52.01. The visible query variants include “bad condensation in bathroom” (12 impressions, position 89.58), “bathroom dampness” (4, position 84), “condensation on bathroom walls” (4, position 93.5), and “how to reduce condensation in bathroom” (2, position 98). The last recorded Google crawl for this page in the 2026-11-01 log was 2026-10-01.

### Competing page evidence

- Health Canada publishes a bathroom moisture/mould checklist that recommends using and maintaining an exhaust fan, checking that it vents outdoors, and running it for at least 30 minutes after bathing. It also provides broader household moisture-control advice.
- RICS explains condensation as water vapour turning to droplets on cold surfaces and lists factors such as poor ventilation and drying clothes indoors.
- The CMHC “Moisture and Air” guide gives practical bathroom ventilation, drying and repair actions.
- Local contractor pages add fan sizing, duct-run and installation-scope detail, but their advice is tied to local codes and services.

### What HomeProjectWise already does well

The HPW condensation guide has a practical diagnostic method:
- a hygrometer baseline and after-shower readings;
- a worked dew-point table;
- a measured 20-minute fan test;
- advice on replacement air, extraction and fan/duct issues;
- a clear condensation-versus-leak distinction and professional escalation;
- references to EPA/HVI guidance.

The measured test is a useful differentiator over a page that only lists “open a window and run the fan”.

### Opportunity and caution

The competitor authorities are stronger on institutional trust and broad public-health guidance. HPW should not try to out-claim them. It should translate primary guidance into a repeatable homeowner test and cite the source beside each threshold.

Fan-run recommendations differ by authority and jurisdiction (for example, Health Canada's checklist says 30 minutes; HPW currently uses HVI's 20-minute guidance). Do not collapse these into one universal number. Explain the cited authority and applicable context.

**Do not rewrite this page before the 2026-11-05 checkpoint.** Its recorded crawl predates later changes, and the current weak positions may not reflect what Google has recrawled.

## 3. Kitchen counter clutter

**Observed HPW signal:** “why does my kitchen clutter keep coming back” has 10 visible impressions at position 10 and maps to the legacy `stop-kitchen-counter-clutter.html` URL; that URL is now a redirect stub. The current destination `kitchen-counter-zone-that-stays-clear.html` has 3 impressions for “kitchen clear”.

### Competing page evidence

- KSL's October 19, 2026 article describes counter clutter as a “boomerang” problem and explains that simply putting things away is not enough; it identifies distinct reasons the counter becomes a landing zone.
- The Home Depot's kitchen-cabinet guide focuses on clearing, grouping items by use and adding organizers such as shelf risers.
- Other broad organization content tends to emphasize decluttering and storage products.

### What HomeProjectWise already does well

The current HPW destination directly answers why clutter returns in its opening, then offers one clear preparation zone, a quick-win reset and a simple routine. It does not require buying organizers before testing the layout. That is a good match for the observed “why does it keep coming back?” intent.

### Required action

No new article and no rewrite are justified by the evidence. Verify the legacy redirect, destination canonical and internal links. All new links should point to the current destination. Preserve the old URL so the query/click history is not discarded.

## 4. Cable clutter meaning and reduction

**Observed HPW signal:** “cable clutter meaning” has 4 visible impressions at position 7.5; the page has 18 page impressions at average position 11.39.

### Competing page evidence

- WIRED's March 2026 guide offers a desk-cable cleanup workflow and product recommendations, drawing on a reviewer's own cable-heavy setup.
- Tom's Guide gives a list of practical products and expert suggestions (ties, clips, raceways, pouches and charging stations).
- HP and HGTV pages show desk-routing steps and organizer/product options.

### What HomeProjectWise already does well

The HPW page defines “cable clutter” explicitly near the top, then maps cables, removes unused ones, separates permanent from temporary connections and keeps frequently changed cables serviceable. This directly serves the definition query and is more maintenance-conscious than hiding every cable in a box.

### Opportunity

Large publishers have product breadth, shopping links and expert quotations. HPW should not imitate their product catalog. Its defensible angle is a free, serviceable system that avoids buying products before auditing the cables. Keep the definition near the top and make the audit/sort/route/label/store sequence easy to scan. No separate definition page.

## 5. Water leak sensor placement

**Observed HPW signal:** “where to place water leak sensors” has 5 visible impressions at position 71.6 and “where to put water leak sensors” has 1 at position 91; page-level average position is 38.2.

### Competing page evidence

- Moen's official Flo Detector placement guide lists common locations (under sinks, under toilets, basements, floor drains, appliances, water heaters, boilers and sump pumps) and explains that its mounting base disables the underside sensors unless the remote disc/cable accessory is installed.
- Vivint and recent specialist guides provide room-by-room placement checklists, including washing machines, dishwashers, water heaters, toilets, basements/crawl spaces and HVAC/sump locations.
- Utility Explained adds prioritisation when the owner has a limited number of sensors and identifies low-value placements.

### What HomeProjectWise already does well

The HPW guide gives six priority locations, says to place the sensor flat at the lowest point, distinguishes an alarm-only sensor from an automatic shut-off valve, and includes testing and maintenance advice. It is not simply a list of rooms.

### Opportunity

The official manufacturer page exposes an important product-specific issue: puck sensors, remote sensing discs and cable accessories can have different active sensing surfaces and mounting requirements. HP should explain that placement must follow the exact sensor manual and avoid generalising one brand's mounting rules. A useful addition after the checkpoint could be a priority order for a limited number of sensors plus a short “where not to place one” section, sourced from manuals and with no unsupported risk ranking.

## 7. What we can and cannot claim

**Supported by this first pass**
- The basement toilet, kitchen clutter, cable clutter, condensation and leak-sensor pages have identifiable search intents and specific competing page formats.
- HPW already has meaningful practical strengths on several of these pages.
- The macerating guide's unreferenced cost ranges deserve a source audit.
- The kitchen redirect needs a migration check.
- Manufacturer-specific leak-sensor mounting details are a potential content gap.

**Not supported**
- “HomeProjectWise beats the strongest 20 websites.”
- Any site-wide competitor score or ranking.
- Competitor traffic, backlink strength or conversion performance.
- Claims that any competitor page is factually wrong without checking its cited sources and local context.
