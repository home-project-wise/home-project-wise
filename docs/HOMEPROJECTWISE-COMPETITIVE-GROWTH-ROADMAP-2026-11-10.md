# HomeProjectWise — Competitive Growth Research & Execution Roadmap

**Prepared:** 2026-11-10  
**Owner:** HomeProjectWise  
**Research scope:** Home improvement, home organization, small-space design, smart-home troubleshooting, project calculators and homeowner decision tools.  
**Markets assumed:** United States first; UK/Canada second where units, product standards and local codes can be handled responsibly.  
**Status:** Research and prioritisation document. It is not authorization to publish every proposed page or to merge visitor-facing changes without the Value Before Publishing gate.

## Executive decision

HomeProjectWise should not try to become a smaller copy of The Spruce, Good Housekeeping, Apartment Therapy or Home Depot. Those competitors have much greater brand reach, content scale, links, product ecosystems and/or institutional trust.

The credible opportunity is to become the best **connected home-project decision workflow** for selected homeowner jobs:

> Diagnose the situation → understand the safe options → calculate materials and user-entered costs → compare trade-offs → know what to do next and when to call a professional.

This is more defensible than publishing generic lists. It joins practical guides, transparent calculators, source-backed limits and a project-level budget in one journey.

### What the evidence says now

- Repository inventory on 2026-11-10: **31 article HTML pages and 7 tool pages** (including the flagship Home Project Calculator, Project Budget Planner and Home Check-up).
- The flagship calculator currently has five tabs: area/volume, paint, flooring/materials, electricity cost and project cost.
- Four library categories exist: Storage & Organization, Home Design, Weekend Projects and Smart Home.
- The latest dated Search Console snapshot available in the repository is the 2026-11-03 intent analysis for 2026-10-06–2026-10-31: 432 property impressions, 1 click, 0.231% CTR and average position 19.96. This is an early baseline, not evidence of competitive traction. It must be refreshed before using it as a current KPI.
- The strongest visible page signals in that window were macerating toilet/basement bathroom (109 impressions, position 11.28), bathroom condensation (78, position 52.01), large wall art (44, position 5.64), musty towels (31, position 24.81), leak-sensor placement (20, position 38.20), evening/motion lighting (19 each, positions 6.53/6.21), smart-plug suitability (19, position 7.05), cable clutter (18, position 11.39) and small-bathroom cleaning (17, position 10.82).
- Important limitation: the current GSC file is not a live November 10 export. Search Console's query table was incomplete/anonymised, and its visible rows represented only a portion of property impressions.

**Strategic implication:** First improve and connect pages that already receive impressions or rank near page one. Do not flood the site with generic high-volume topics where domain authority is insufficient.

## 1. Competitive landscape

These competitors are grouped by the job they compete for. Their site-wide traffic is not directly comparable to HomeProjectWise traffic, and their business models differ.

### Tier A — Large editorial publishers

| Competitor | September 2026 estimated visits | Strength to learn from | What HomeProjectWise should do differently |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Good Housekeeping | 23.73M | Editorial reach, consumer testing/reviews, broad household coverage | Do not imitate breadth. Build narrower decision tools and show assumptions/sources at the exact decision point. |
| The Spruce | 10.33M | Broad how-to library, practical packaging, topical coverage | Win selected problem clusters with connected guides, calculators and diagnostic decision paths. |
| Real Simple | 7.85M | Accessible household advice and organization topics | Target specific room/constraint situations rather than broad generic organization terms. |
| Better Homes & Gardens | 6.53M | Visual presentation, room/project inspiration, wide home coverage | Pair relevant imagery with measurable steps, formulas, costs entered by the reader and clear limits. |
| Apartment Therapy | 3.01M | Small-space/renter context, room-led organization, before/after storytelling | Use real constraints (rental, no drilling, narrow rooms, shared spaces) and turn them into measurable solutions. |

Traffic figures are third-party estimates for total site visits, not official analytics, not organic-only traffic, and not the traffic of a single article. Source pages and dates are listed in Section 10.

### Tier B — Project, repair and DIY publishers

| Competitor | Competitive strength | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| This Old House | Expert-led repair and renovation explanations, project videos and cost-intent coverage | Explain the whole project scope, sequence, dependencies and professional boundaries. |
| Family Handyman | Project-first steps, tools/materials and practical execution | Make preparation, tools, skill, time, checkpoints and finish checks obvious before the reader starts. |
| Bob Vila | DIY taxonomy, maintenance, buying guides and editorial trust presentation | Separate advice from buying comparisons; explain recommendation criteria and commercial disclosures. |
| HGTV | Strong visual storytelling and project inspiration | Use a visual before/after or decision diagram when it clarifies the task, but keep the answer first. |
| Houzz | Image-led inspiration, project exploration and professional ecosystem | Help visitors decide what fits their room and constraints before they spend or hire. |

### Tier C — Calculators, retailers and transaction competitors

| Competitor | Strength | Opportunity for HomeProjectWise |
|---|---|---|
| Home Depot / Lowe's | Product specifications, inventory, project calculators and shopping journeys | Stay retailer-neutral; calculate from the exact product label and the visitor's own price. |
| Homewyse | Cost breakdowns and project estimate intent | Provide editable quote inputs, scope checklists, assumptions and uncertainty instead of pretending to know a universal local price. |
| Inch Calculator | Broad specialist calculator library and conversions | Make outputs purchase-ready: units, rounding, waste, formula, worked example and limitations. |
| Omni Calculator / Calculator.net | Breadth and familiar quick-calculation experience | Avoid shallow calculator sprawl; offer a complete home-project workflow with related guide and budget hand-off. |
| IKEA | Product discovery and room-planning ecosystem at enormous scale | Do not compete on catalogue size; solve independent measurement, fit and quantity questions before purchase. |

### Tier D — Primary authorities and specialist references

- EPA, ENERGY STAR, Department of Energy, HVI, manufacturer installation manuals and relevant standards bodies.
- These are not ordinary SEO competitors. They are the evidence base that must support safety, ventilation, moisture, electrical-load, energy and product-compatibility claims.
- Reddit and Home Improvement Stack Exchange are useful for finding recurring edge cases and reader language, but anecdotes must never be treated as standards or proof.

### Relative competitive position

HomeProjectWise cannot currently compete on:
- domain authority, brand recognition, breadth or number of indexed URLs;
- independent lab/product testing, professional trade credentials or local contractor pricing;
- a large photo library, community or retail inventory.

HomeProjectWise can compete on:
- a connected guide + calculator + project budget journey;
- exact product-label inputs and user-entered prices rather than fabricated live prices;
- transparent formulas, rounding and uncertainty;
- “do not proceed / call a professional” boundaries;
- privacy-first calculations that run in the browser;
- narrowly focused, reproducible homeowner tests and original diagrams.

## 3. Current HomeProjectWise inventory and strengths

### Current site footprint

**31 article pages** (repository count, 2026-11-10) span:
- Storage & Organization: entryway, cable clutter, kitchen counter, home reset, mail/paper.
- Home Design: calm room, evening lighting, small living room, rug sizing, wallpaper planning.
- Weekend Projects / repairs: damp crawl space, dehumidifier sizing, drafty door, wobbly furniture, bathroom condensation, bathroom fan, fan dripping, macerating toilet, wall art, small bathroom, paint finish and related tasks.
- Smart Home: smart-plug suitability, lighting, motion lighting, Wi-Fi outage, offline devices, privacy and thermostat compatibility.

**7 tool pages**:
1. Home Project Calculator
2. Project Budget Planner
3. Home Check-up
4. Rug Size Calculator
5. Crawl Space Dehumidifier Size Calculator
6. Bathroom Exhaust Fan Size Calculator
7. Smart Plug Load Check

### What is already strong

1. The site has a clear practical-home promise and four task/topic categories.
2. The homepage provides problem-led routes and gives the flagship calculator a prominent role.
3. The flagship calculator uses visitor-entered measurements, product coverage, tariffs and prices; its page says calculations run in the browser and are not sent to the server.
4. Existing guides often contain a quick answer, worked examples, original SVG diagrams, primary sources, safety limits and links to relevant tools.
5. Several articles already have distinctive angles: humidity/dew-point measurement and fan tests; wallpaper drop/yield arithmetic; bathroom fan static pressure/duct constraints; smart-plug load checks; and cable management that remains serviceable.
6. The project has a Value Before Publishing quality law and an editorial policy that avoids fabricated authorship, credentials and hands-on tests.

### Where the current product still falls short

These are repository-level findings and priorities, not claims that every page is poor:

- **Search traction is still very early.** The dated GSC snapshot has 1 click from 432 impressions. This is too little data to infer winners or monetize at scale.
- **Topic depth is uneven.** There are 31 guides, but some clusters have only a handful of supporting pages. A category card is not the same as a complete topical hub.
- **The site mixes several reader jobs.** Storage/organization, interior layout, repairs, smart-home reliability and project estimating can coexist, but each needs a clear promise and separate intent map so the site does not look unfocused.
- **Some strong guides are isolated from a complete user journey.** The best opportunity is to connect diagnosis, guide, calculator, project budget, printable result and related next step.
- **Calculator coverage is not yet a moat.** Area, paint, flooring, energy and budget are useful, but competitors already offer generic calculators. The value must come from correct material/package arithmetic, project-wide totals, comparisons and traceable assumptions.
- **Product comparison and Wallpaper UI work are not signed off.** The wallpaper planning proposal explicitly identifies two-product comparison, exact sale-unit costs and the project-budget adapter as important differentiators. PR #269 remains a separate implementation track and must not be represented as complete until its acceptance tests and visual review pass.
- **Visual evidence needs stronger image readiness checks.** PR #273 was opened after manual review found lazy-loaded card images missing from some CI captures. Do not use old green screenshot checks as proof that every image was visibly loaded.
- **Authority is transparent but limited.** The editorial policy openly says guides are AI-assisted and not individually reviewed by licensed tradespeople. That honesty should remain. High-consequence guidance needs stronger primary-source support and, where feasible, a real qualified reviewer—not invented credentials.
- **Brand/domain authority is limited by the GitHub Pages project URL.** A custom domain may improve brand trust and consistency, but requires owner-controlled purchase/DNS and should not outrank content/tool improvements.
- **Image library quality and relevance remain a separate workstream.** Do not start a bulk image replacement from this roadmap. Keep image work separate and inspect each asset against its article section.

### Priority B — New content clusters with a distinct advantage

**1. Bathroom ventilation and moisture (best near-term cluster fit)**  
Existing assets: condensation guide, bathroom fan selection guide, fan-size calculator, dripping-fan article, musty-towel article, small-bathroom guide.

Potential additions only after SERP validation:
- Bathroom exhaust fan not pulling air: a safe diagnostic flow (grille/filter, tissue test limitations, replacement air, duct blockage, outside termination, professional escalation).

- Bathroom fan replacement checklist: exact dimensions, duct diameter, static pressure, sones, continuous-use rating, wiring/permit boundary.

Differentiator: reader can diagnose, size airflow, understand duct constraints and know when installation needs a professional.

**2. Material quantity and project budgeting (highest product differentiation)**  
Existing assets: Home Project Calculator, Budget Planner, wallpaper article/engine, paint and flooring tabs.

Potential additions:
- Tile quantity calculator and guide: tile size, grout joint, layout/cuts, box coverage, room-by-room rounding and user-entered box price. This is the strongest calculator candidate from the current public keyword signal; verify US SERP and exact calculation rules first.
- Flooring buying worksheet: plank/box coverage, pattern/layout, stairs/closets, waste assumption, room-by-room rounding and price per sale unit.
- Paint calculator guide: wall area, doors/windows policy, coats, actual label coverage, primer and tin-size rounding; update existing content rather than create a competing calculator URL.
- Project quote comparison worksheet: same scope, material quantities, labor, disposal, permits, delivery, tax, contingency and exclusions. It should prepare a fair comparison, not invent a market quote.

Differentiator: exact product packaging and total project cost, not only a square-foot result.

**3. Smart-home reliability and safe compatibility (narrow specialist cluster)**  
Existing assets: smart plug load check, offline devices, Wi-Fi outage test, privacy check, smart lighting and thermostat without C-wire.

Potential additions:
- Smart device keeps disconnecting: decision tree by Wi-Fi band, signal, router settings, cloud dependency, firmware and power—careful not to give unsafe electrical advice.
- Smart home offline plan: which automations still work locally, which fail without cloud/internet, and how to preserve manual control.
- Thermostat compatibility pre-check: HVAC type, terminal labels, C-wire/power adapter and clear stop conditions; never infer compatibility from wire colours alone.

Differentiator: troubleshoot the system and failure mode, not just review gadgets.

**4. Small-space / storage (selective; do not chase generic volume)**  
Existing assets: entryway, counter, cable, mail/paper, reset, small living room, rug, small bathroom.

Potential additions:
- No-closet entryway storage for renters: measured wall/door/floor constraints and no-drill options.
- Shared small bathroom storage: reach, humidity, cleaning access and person-specific zones.
- Desk drawer organization: only if an original measured layout, item-fit method and printable plan add something beyond Apartment Therapy's broad inspiration.
- Under-sink storage around pipes: exact pipe clearance, shutoff access and leak tray; useful, but only after verifying SERP demand and ensuring the article includes a measured diagram.

Do not publish generic “100 organization hacks”, generic closet ideas or a broad listicle. Those are directly contested by established publishers and do not exploit our calculator strength.

### Ranked calculator bets

1. **Tile quantity and cost calculator — highest new calculator priority.** Build from exact box coverage and tile dimensions; account for grout/joint and layout/cuts with visible assumptions; round to whole boxes; compare two products only when units/currencies match; export/print shopping list; hand off total to Budget Planner. Validate SERP, formulas and product-label conventions before implementation.
2. **Finish Wallpaper Calculator integration.** The engine exists in the current workstream, but UI integration, two-product comparison, adapter tests and Wallpaper-tab browser/visual review must pass. This is already in progress; do not create a competing standalone URL.
3. **Connect flagship outputs to Project Budget Planner.** This may create more value than another standalone tool: quantities from paint/flooring/wallpaper should become budget line items with explicit unit and price source.
4. **Improve paint and flooring panels.** Make pack/tin rounding, multiple rooms, coats/coverage, waste assumptions and product-specific sale units explicit. Do not silently treat user coverage as universal.
5. **Quote comparison worksheet.** User-entered quotes, identical scope, exclusions, labor, permits, delivery, disposal and tax. This can attract high-intent visitors and make the budget planner more useful.

### Do not build yet

- Generic concrete, gravel, mulch or electrical calculators just because a keyword study reports high volume.
- A live retailer price scraper without licensing, stable product matching, country/currency handling and a freshness process.
- A calculator that presents a planning estimate as a contractor quote or code/safety approval.
- A second tool URL for a calculation already served by the flagship calculator.

## 6. Design, UX, trust and technical gaps

### Design and information architecture

**Keep:** calm editorial visual language, problem-led homepage routes, four category hubs, visible calculator CTA, responsive cards and a dedicated tools index.

**Improve next:**
1. Every article card should show a clear task outcome, not just a broad category label. Use time/difficulty only when substantiated.
2. Make the article page answer visible before the first long explanatory section: quick answer, who this is for, what is needed, safety/stop rule and a clear tool CTA.
3. Add a compact “decision summary” table to comparison/choice articles; use a diagnostic flow for troubleshooting articles; use a calculation worksheet for material articles. Do not force every article into one template.
4. Standardize article metadata: author identity that is truthful, meaningful last-reviewed date only when reviewed, primary sources, correction route, AI image disclosure and affiliate disclosure where relevant.
5. Keep related links contextual: diagnosis → repair → prevention → relevant calculator. Avoid a generic repeated “latest articles” list.
6. Add a visible “what this tool does not decide” statement near every high-consequence calculator result.
7. Review category balance by reader job, not by forcing equal article counts. The Storage cluster is currently smaller than Weekend Projects; only grow it where search demand and original value justify it.

### Trust and expertise

- Keep the current honest disclosure that content is AI-assisted and not individually reviewed by licensed tradespeople.
- For electrical, moisture/mould, ventilation, structural loads and code-sensitive advice, use primary authorities and manufacturer manuals near the relevant claims.
- Prioritise a qualified external reviewer for a small number of high-consequence cornerstone pages if feasible. Display name, qualification, scope and review date only after genuine review and consent.
- Add a transparent methodology for calculators: formula, source, units, rounding, sample calculation, supported cases, unsupported cases and last logic review.
- Add genuine editorial corrections and version history for tools; do not fake author credentials, first-hand testing, user case studies or product tests.

### Visual quality and images

- The current visual QA workflow captures configured pages and screenshots, but PR #273 addresses a discovered lazy-image blind spot. Do not treat the old 400/400 run as image-completeness sign-off.
- Keep image selection/replacement as its own authorised phase. This roadmap does not authorize bulk image generation or replacement.
- Require every new visual to explain a real task: measurement, placement, wiring/duct path, product label, before/after, layout or decision—not generic decoration.
- Add original short videos only where motion explains a technique or diagnosis; do not add videos as a quota.

### Technical SEO and discovery

- Maintain canonical URLs, redirects, generated sitemap/feed/indexes and internal links.
- Refresh GSC/Bing indexing and query/page data. Separate impressions, clicks, CTR and average position by country/device/page/query.
- Build one primary URL per intent and a query-to-URL map to prevent cannibalisation.
- Measure real Core Web Vitals and mobile performance. The automated visual QA is not a Lighthouse/field-data substitute.
- Evaluate custom domain only as a brand/trust decision with owner approval; it is not a near-term ranking shortcut.
- Add structured data only where it accurately represents visible page content; do not expect schema to guarantee rich results.

## 8. Measurement plan and decision rules

### Baseline to refresh

The 2026-11-03 report contains a 26-day window (2026-10-06–2026-10-31): 432 impressions, 1 click, 0.231% CTR and average position 19.96. Treat this only as a historical baseline. Capture a fresh 28-day and 90-day window before evaluating any initiative.

### KPIs

**Search**
- GSC clicks, impressions, CTR and average position by canonical URL, query, country and device.
- Indexed/valid URL count and sitemap discovery/crawl status.
- Non-branded query coverage by cluster; avoid using only domain-wide averages.

**Engagement and tool value**
- Guide → calculator click-through.
- Calculator start, valid completion, error/abandonment and result-to-budget hand-off rates.
- Print/download/summary actions if implemented.
- Related-guide click-through and return visits, if consent-compliant analytics supports it.

**Quality**
- Source coverage and dated claims.
- Calculation test coverage, browser/mobile/keyboard/accessibility checks.
- Image load/alt checks and human screenshot review.
- No unresolved critical safety or privacy defects.

### Decision rules

- Do not celebrate search-volume estimates as traffic.
- Do not publish a page without a distinct query intent, a reader outcome and evidence that existing URLs do not already satisfy it.
- Do not expand a cluster solely because a competitor gets traffic from a broad term.
- Do not use one click or a handful of impressions to claim an SEO win.
- Review changes after sufficient recrawl and a meaningful observation window; annotate major site changes.
- Keep the current Value Before Publishing gate as the release authority.
