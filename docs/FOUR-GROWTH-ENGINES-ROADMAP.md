# HomeProjectWise — Four Growth Engines Operating Roadmap

**Date:** 2026-10-31  
**Status:** Proposed operating roadmap; review required before execution  
**Scope:** Search visibility, search-result clicks, non-search acquisition, and trust/answer-engine discoverability  
**Governing rule:** The complete 16-article Value Before Publishing law (v1.0) remains mandatory. This roadmap does not weaken or replace it.

## 1. Strategic objective

Move HomeProjectWise from a publishing-led workflow to an evidence-led growth system. The goal is not to maximize article count. The goal is to attract relevant visitors, help them complete a real home-related task, earn trust, and create sustainable monetization opportunities without sacrificing usefulness.

The four engines reinforce one another:

1. **Search visibility:** earn relevant impressions in Google and Bing.
2. **Click and satisfaction:** turn qualified impressions into clicks and useful on-site outcomes.
3. **External distribution:** earn relevant visits and referrals without waiting for search engines.
4. **Trust and reference-worthiness:** publish accurate, attributable, useful material that people and answer systems can understand and cite.

A measurement and learning loop governs all four.

## 2. Non-negotiable operating constraints

- No new article batch or mass production.
- No near-duplicate pages or keyword-only pages.
- Preserve the existing minimum opportunity score of **85/100** for any proposed new page or major content investment.
- Improve existing pages first when evidence supports the opportunity.
- Do not change a title merely to chase CTR when ranking, intent match, or page quality is the primary problem.
- Do not invent search volume, positions, traffic, backlinks, tests, citations, or revenue.
- Distinguish unavailable data from zero data.
- No live publishing or merging as part of this strategy document.
- Every visitor-facing change must pass the complete Value Before Publishing gate and include evidence in its PR.
- Keep the current editorial freeze in force unless the project owner explicitly changes it through the established process.

## 3. Engine 1 — Google and Bing search visibility

### 3.1 Build a page-and-query opportunity map

Create one row per important existing URL, with these fields:

- Canonical URL and page type (homepage, guide, tool, resource).
- Primary visitor problem and intended search intent.
- Google Search Console: clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, top queries, and date range.
- Bing Webmaster: clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, and query/page data where available.
- Indexing/canonical status and any detected technical issue.
- Internal links in and out.
- Relevant competitor URLs actually reviewed and review date.
- Current page value and its specific differentiator.
- Affiliate / non-affiliate classification where relevant.
- Opportunity score, evidence confidence, next action, owner, and review date.

Do not compare average positions without checking query mix, country, device, and date range. Do not combine Google and Bing as if their metrics were identical.

### 3.2 Classify pages by action

- **Improve now:** meaningful impressions or a clear, evidence-backed technical/content opportunity.
- **Diagnose first:** low visibility or uncertain intent; investigate indexing, competition, demand, and content usefulness before editing.
- **Monitor:** insufficient data to justify a change.
- **Consolidate or retire from investment:** overlap, weak distinct value, or no defensible role. Any removal/redirect decision requires URL-level review and owner approval.

### 3.3 Initial investigation queue (not a ranking claim)

Start by auditing the existing homepage and the existing pages/research threads concerning:
- Bathroom condensation.
- Basement toilet / macerating toilet.
- Crawl-space humidity and dehumidifier sizing.
- Existing practical tools and calculators, if present and functional.

These are investigation candidates based on existing project work, not claims that they currently rank or have proven demand. Confirm their actual URLs and data before prioritizing.

### 3.4 Homepage review

Assess whether the first screen quickly communicates:
- What HomeProjectWise helps people do.
- Who the site is for.
- Which useful guides and tools are available.
- Why a visitor should trust the guidance.

Keep the core topic clear, but describe the actual product/site experience rather than stuffing keywords. Make resource and tool pathways obvious. Validate the page against real user tasks, not aesthetics alone.

### 3.5 Internal linking

Build topic pathways only where they help a visitor progress from diagnosis to decision to action. Use descriptive anchors, verify destinations, avoid orphaning important pages, and do not add links solely to increase link counts.

## 4. Engine 2 — Improve search-result CTR and post-click satisfaction

CTR work is conditional on the diagnosis.

- If position/visibility is weak, investigate content usefulness, intent match, competition, indexing, and internal links first.
- If a page earns meaningful impressions at a competitive position but underperforms on CTR, test a title/description hypothesis.
- If clicks arrive but visitors do not use the guide/tool, inspect the opening answer, page promise, mobile experience, speed, and task completion.

For each title/description experiment, record:
- URL, target query cluster, country/device where possible.
- Baseline date range and metrics.
- The exact change and reason.
- A comparison window with adequate data.
- Other changes that could confound the result.
- Decision: keep, revert, or continue observing.

Write titles that accurately reflect the page and its unique benefit. Descriptions should explain the useful outcome, not make unsupported promises. Put the direct answer near the beginning when that best satisfies the search intent. Google may construct snippets and title links from page content; keep title, headings, visible content, and structured data consistent.

## 5. Engine 3 — Acquisition beyond search

### 5.1 Focused resource outreach

Select **two** genuinely relevant resource targets for the first outreach experiment. Suitable categories may include home-maintenance resource libraries, housing/tenant advice resources, facilities or property-management guidance, and specialist educational resources.

For each target:
- Verify that the page and audience are real and relevant.
- Identify one specific resource or tool that genuinely helps their readers.
- Explain the reader benefit in a short, personalized message.
- Ask for feedback or offer the resource; do not demand a backlink.
- Track contact date, response, referral visits, and useful feedback.
- Stop or revise after evidence, not after sending a large batch.

### 5.2 Shareable utility assets

Prioritize an existing tool only after verifying that it solves a real task and works correctly. Add only useful sharing affordances, such as a copyable result/URL, print-friendly output, or a clear explanation that can be shared.

### 5.3 Embeddable tools — demand-gated

Do not build an embed system speculatively. First validate repeated use, user requests, or partner interest. Then prototype a minimal, accessible embed with clear attribution, responsive behavior, security boundaries, and documented limitations. Measure embeds, referrals, and task completion.

### 5.4 Other distribution experiments

Test one channel at a time where the audience already seeks help: specialist communities, relevant newsletters, partner resource pages, or short visual explainers. Follow community rules; contribute a useful answer first; avoid spam and mass posting. Record effort and qualified visits so low-yield channels can be stopped.

## 6. Engine 4 — Trust, original assets, and answer-engine discoverability

### 6.1 Citation-worthy assets

Develop small, defensible assets from real research or calculations:
- A transparent comparison table with dated sources and explicit criteria.
- A calculator with a published method, assumptions, units, and edge-case behavior.
- A checklist or diagnostic flow that helps a visitor decide what to do next.
- A compact dataset only when values can be collected, checked, and maintained responsibly.

Do not label a table or estimate original data unless HomeProjectWise actually collected or calculated it and can explain how.

### 6.2 Evidence and editorial trust

For claims that affect safety, cost, compliance, or purchasing:
- Prefer authoritative primary sources and manufacturer documentation where appropriate.
- Cite the source close to the claim.
- Record publication/review dates for changing information.
- Explain assumptions, uncertainty, and when professional help is appropriate.
- Never imply hands-on testing unless it happened.

### 6.3 Answer-engine readiness

Make pages easy to understand, verify, and quote:
- Answer the real question plainly.
- Use descriptive headings and meaningful tables/lists where helpful.
- Identify the source and date for important facts.
- Keep visible content consistent with structured data.
- Use valid schema only when the page genuinely qualifies; schema is not a ranking or citation guarantee.
- Ensure important resources are crawlable and internally discoverable.

Do not create pages solely to target AI answers, and do not promise placement in ChatGPT, AI Overviews, or other answer systems. Track attributable referral sources where available and treat unmeasurable exposure as unknown.

## 7. Measurement system

### 7.1 Separate acquisition from engagement

**Search:** impressions, clicks, CTR, query mix, page-level performance, indexing status.  
**Non-search:** referral sessions/visits by source, outreach responses, partner referrals.  
**On-site utility:** tool starts, completed calculations/tasks, key CTA interactions, errors, and repeat usage where measurement can be implemented with appropriate privacy.  
**Business:** qualified affiliate clicks and approved revenue data, only when the relevant programs and tracking are active.

GoatCounter currently provides a daily traffic snapshot workflow when its API key is configured. It does not replace Search Console or Bing query data. Keep these sources separate.

### 7.2 Instrumentation acceptance

Before interpreting performance:
- Verify the GoatCounter property and page tracking on representative pages.
- Confirm the daily report's latest status is successful; unavailable or error is not zero traffic.
- Verify UTM/referrer capture for controlled outreach where appropriate.
- Confirm Search Console and Bing data can be exported or accessed for page/query analysis.
- Define privacy-conscious events only for meaningful user actions; avoid collecting personal data unnecessarily.

## 9. Opportunity scoring (85/100 minimum retained)

Use the existing 85/100 threshold for proposed new pages and major content investments. For existing-page improvements, use the same rubric as a prioritization aid, not an automatic publish authorization.

Suggested 100-point rubric:
- Evidence of real demand / existing search or user signals: 25.
- Severity and clarity of visitor problem: 20.
- Distinct, demonstrable added value: 20.
- Credible competitive gap: 15.
- Feasibility and ability to verify quality: 10.
- Measurement and learning potential: 10.

A score cannot compensate for a fatal issue: inaccurate or unsafe guidance, no genuine visitor value, duplication, unsupported claims, or failure of the mandatory law. Record confidence and missing evidence beside the score. A score is a decision aid, not proof of demand or guaranteed success.

## 12. Definition of success

Success is not number of articles shipped. It is evidence that:
- More relevant pages earn qualified search visibility.
- Search clicks improve where the ranking and intent diagnosis justify CTR work.
- Visitors complete useful tasks on guides and tools.
- Relevant non-search referrals begin to appear.
- HomeProjectWise creates assets others have a concrete reason to reference.
- The team can explain what worked, what did not, and what to do next using real evidence.

**Operating principle:** Build less blindly. Learn faster. Improve what already has a reason to win. Earn every visitor.
