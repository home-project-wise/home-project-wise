# Operational Quality and Evidence Standard

This standard applies to planning, research, content production, development, review, and post-publication improvement for HomeProjectWise. It is a required decision rule, not an optional checklist.

## 1. Evidence, assumptions, costs, and limits

- Cite or link sources when factual claims, recommendations, comparisons, or decisions depend on external evidence.
- Clearly distinguish verified facts, estimates, assumptions, and unknowns.
- State material costs, trade-offs, constraints, and limitations where relevant.
- Never invent analytics, test results, competitor findings, citations, user outcomes, or completed work. If a value has not been checked, say so.

## 2. Measure and improve from real data

- Review available search impressions, clicks, click-through rate, queries, indexing status, and site-usage data when relevant to the task.
- Use observed data to adjust priorities and improve existing pages; do not present guesses as measurements.
- Record the date range, data source, and relevant limitations when reporting results.
- If results are weak or absent, diagnose likely causes and gather evidence before repeating the same approach or scaling production.

## 3. Test before scaling

- Do not create dozens of similar pages until a smaller set demonstrates distinct reader value and a credible reason to exist.
- A published page is not, by itself, evidence of success. Evaluate usefulness, differentiation, quality, and available performance signals.
- Check for overlap with existing pages, search intent, factual accuracy, practical usefulness, and whether the page offers something meaningfully better or different from alternatives.

## 4. Mandatory pre-PR decision record

Before opening or requesting approval for every pull request, document:

1. **Problem:** What specific user or product problem is being addressed?
2. **Reader benefit:** What concrete new benefit will the visitor receive?
3. **Differentiation:** Why should someone choose this page or solution over existing pages and competing alternatives? Name the meaningful distinction; do not make unsupported competitor claims.
4. **Evidence and assumptions:** What sources and observed data support the decision? What remains assumed or unknown?
5. **Validation:** What checks or tests were performed, what were their results, and what was not tested?
6. **Costs and limits:** What relevant costs, trade-offs, dependencies, risks, and limitations apply?
7. **Success measure:** What signal will be monitored after release, from which source, and over what review period, where practical?

If a section is not applicable, state why. Do not fill gaps with invented claims.

## 5. Mandatory acceptance gate

If the team cannot explain the visitor's benefit and identify a credible, evidence-based reason to choose this work over available alternatives, **do not publish or merge it in its current form**. Redesign it, gather evidence, or defer it.

A PR must not be treated as ready merely because the code runs, the page exists, or the content is indexed. Technical correctness and reader value are separate requirements.

## 6. Post-release review

- After publication, inspect real search and usage data when enough data is available; note the actual review date and period.
- Compare observed results with the stated goal. Avoid claiming causation from correlation or drawing strong conclusions from very small samples.
- Improve, consolidate, or retire underperforming work based on diagnosis. Do not continue producing more pages by the same method without understanding why results are missing.
- If access to Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, or analytics is unavailable, explicitly record that limitation and request the required evidence rather than claiming a review occurred.

## 7. Team working rule

All contributors and assistants, including manual and automated workflows, must apply this standard during research, planning, implementation, and review—not only after work is complete. Advice to a teammate should be concrete and actionable. Work not actually performed must be described as proposed or pending, never as completed.

**Core principle:** We are not trying to create the largest number of pages. We are creating pages that deserve to be found, used, and revisited.
