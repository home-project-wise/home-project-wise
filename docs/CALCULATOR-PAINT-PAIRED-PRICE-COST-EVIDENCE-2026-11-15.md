# Paint paired retail-price and coverage evidence — 2026-11-15

**Owner:** ChatGPT — source research and data quality  
**Purpose:** Determine whether paint material cost can be estimated by pairing a specific retail SKU price with that same product's coverage, rather than multiplying unrelated market-wide price and coverage extremes.  
**Status:** Research snapshot; no paint price or cost default is approved.

## Why pair price and coverage

A reliable cost-per-area sample must pair:
1. the exact product/SKU and package volume;
2. the observed retail price for that same SKU and market;
3. the coverage/rendement for that exact product, preferably its TDS;
4. the coat count/system and substrate conditions.

Do **not** multiply the cheapest paint price in a market by the most optimistic coverage from a different premium product. That creates a fictitious product. Do not convert one retailer SKU into a category price. The values below are paired SKU observations and should remain product-specific.

For a product with price P per litre and rate R m²/L **per coat**, the two-finish-coat material cost is:

cost per m² = (2 × P) ÷ R

If the manufacturer gives a complete two-coat system yield S m²/L, cost per m² = P ÷ S. No extra 10% allowance is added. Primer, labour, tools, delivery and taxes not explicitly included are excluded.

## Tax, offer and seller limitations

- Retail pages often do not state tax inclusion in the retrieved excerpt. Germany explicitly says VAT included; UK Screwfix pages say prices include VAT, but this table uses Crown/Wickes, Dulux and HMG pages whose tax wording should be verified before publishing. US and Canadian sales taxes are typically added by location at checkout, but the exact page should be checked.
- Promotions and member-only prices are kept separate from regular/list prices. Expired promotions (for example RONA SICO promotion to 30 Sep 2026 and Boero promotion to 9 Nov 2026) are not treated as current offers.
- Price observations are exact SKU/retailer pairs, not category-wide national price averages. A single product/retailer observation cannot be used as the country's default.
- Shipping, delivery, local store stock, tinting surcharges, special colours and VAT/sales tax are excluded unless stated.
- Product grades vary widely (budget, standard, trade, premium, specialist washable). A single unqualified low–high range hides this difference.

## Decision

The paired-SKU method is the right calculation method for paint cost. It is more defensible than multiplying an unrelated price-per-litre range by a coverage range. However, this first snapshot has only 1–3 matched product rows per country and mixes product tiers, sales/clearance prices and tax treatment. **No country-level paint cost default is approved yet.**

### Required before release
1. At least three current, exact SKU price-and-coverage pairs per market, from at least two independent retailers and two manufacturers; ideally three of each.
2. Exact same SKU's coverage basis verified against its TDS or product page; no generic calculator default substituted.
3. Separate budget/standard/premium product classes or explicitly disclose a broad product-sample envelope.
4. Verify package size, sale/list price, member eligibility, stock, seller, tax inclusion and retrieval date.
5. Calculate two-coat cost per m² from each paired SKU; then present the sample's LOW–HIGH envelope without averaging and preserve every source link.
6. Keep primer, labour, tools, delivery, tax and additional coats outside the material-only total unless separately sourced.

No price registry or assumptions registry is changed by this document.
