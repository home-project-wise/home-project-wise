## 1. Reader problem and outcome
A US homeowner or renter wants to prepare vulnerable plumbing before a hard freeze, recognize a frozen line before it ruptures, thaw only when it is safe to do so, and act quickly if a pipe bursts. The reader should leave with:
- A practical inspection checklist for vulnerable pipes (exterior walls, garages, attics, crawl spaces, basements, under-sink cabinets and outdoor hose connections).
- Prevention actions before and during cold weather, with clear limits on temperature and dripping advice.
- A safe decision path that distinguishes a suspected frozen pipe from a pipe that has already split.
- A short burst-pipe emergency sequence, including water shutoff, electrical danger, plumber escalation and damage documentation.
- Clear instructions about when not to attempt DIY thawing.

### Safety and factual controls
- Do not say pipes universally freeze at 20°F or exactly 32°F. Explain that risk depends on exposure, duration, airflow, insulation, location and the plumbing configuration.
- Do not present 55°F as a guarantee that every pipe is safe. Attribute it as Red Cross/IBHS guidance for an unoccupied home, explain that unusually exposed pipes may need additional protection, and tell renters to follow landlord/property-manager instructions and applicable lease requirements.
- Dripping is not a universal requirement for every faucet. Tie it to faucets serving exposed/vulnerable pipes during very cold weather; explain the water-use trade-off.
- Drain pools/irrigation only according to the manufacturer or installer. Never recommend putting antifreeze into irrigation or plumbing lines unless the system manufacturer specifically directs it.
- Do not advise DIY thawing if a pipe may have split, water is near electrical equipment, the pipe is near a gas line, the pipe is inaccessible, or the reader is unsure. No torches, open flames, propane/kerosene heaters or charcoal devices.
- Reconcile differing thawing instructions conservatively. Red Cross says keep the faucet open and apply gentle heat; Lowe’s advises shutting off the water supply while working and checking for a rupture first. The final article must distinguish a confirmed burst (shut the main water supply) from a suspected frozen pipe with no sign of a rupture, and should direct uncertain cases to a licensed plumber rather than present conflicting instructions as one universal rule.
- For a burst pipe: prioritize stopping water; do not touch wet electrical equipment or use electrical devices in wet areas. If water is near outlets, appliances, wiring or the electrical panel, do not enter unsafe standing water or attempt to operate equipment from a wet location; seek qualified electrical/emergency help.
- Insurance coverage is policy- and cause-specific. Separate sudden/accidental burst-pipe damage from sewer backup and flood; tell readers to contact their insurer and preserve photos/receipts.
- Do not claim HomeProjectWise has personally tested thawing products, pipes, insulation or heat cables.

## 7. Proposed differentiation and solution
A candidate page should use one clear decision framework rather than a generic winter tips list:

1. **Before the freeze:** a location-based inspection checklist; outdoor hose bibs/hoses; vulnerable indoor supply lines; pipe insulation and draft sealing; heat/absence plan; when to ask a plumber about heat cable or pipe relocation.
2. **During a cold snap:** which cabinet doors to open, when a slight drip is appropriate, and how to protect children from cleaners stored under sinks.
3. **Suspected freeze:** symptoms, how to check safely, what not to touch, and a conservative “DIY only if visible, accessible, apparently intact and dry” boundary.
4. **Confirmed burst:** a prominent emergency sequence (main water shutoff; electrical/gas hazard stop; plumber; protect belongings; document damage; insurer; dry/repair).
5. **After thaw/repair:** inspect other faucets/lines, restore water slowly only under safe/professional guidance, monitor for leaks, repair insulation/air leaks and record vulnerable locations.

### Safety resolution — final editorial rule

The final article must present two different branches:

**A. Suspected frozen pipe, no visible rupture and no wet/electrical hazard:** Red Cross recommends keeping the affected faucet open and applying gentle heat only to an accessible section using its listed methods; never use an open flame. Stop and call a licensed plumber if the location is inaccessible, cannot be safely reached, or does not thaw. If any sign of a split, leak, or uncertainty appears, do not continue DIY thawing; switch to the burst/uncertain branch and call a plumber.

**B. Confirmed or reasonably suspected burst:** shut off the main water supply if it is safe to reach; avoid wet electrical equipment, appliances and standing water near electricity; call a licensed plumber; protect belongings only if safe; photograph damage and contact the insurer. Do not operate electrical equipment in wet areas. If water is near wiring, outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, do not enter the hazard area to reach a valve or breaker; seek qualified emergency assistance.

This reconciles the apparent difference between Red Cross's thawing instructions for a frozen line and consumer/retail guidance warning to inspect for rupture before thawing. It avoids telling a homeowner to thaw a potentially ruptured line.
