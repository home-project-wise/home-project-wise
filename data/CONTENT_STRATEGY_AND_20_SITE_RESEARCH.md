# HomeProjectWise: content strategy and execution brief

**Prepared:** 2026-10-24  
**Purpose:** Establish the editorial boundary and a practical research/implementation sequence before major site changes. This is a strategy brief, not a claim that a full 20-site crawl, keyword-volume study, or live UX audit has already been completed.

## 1. Editorial promise

**HomeProjectWise helps homeowners solve practical home problems, make safer and smarter improvement decisions, and choose useful tools or devices.**

The site must not become a second Home Organization Ideas. The other site owns organizing possessions, decluttering, closet/drawer/pantry storage ideas, and room-by-room tidying. HomeProjectWise may cover a room only when the core intent is repair, installation, troubleshooting, comfort, energy use, safety, or a clearly defined DIY improvement.

### Primary editorial pillars

1. **Home Repairs & Troubleshooting** — diagnose common faults, understand causes, safe first checks, repair options, prevention, and when to call a qualified professional.
2. **Smart Home & Lighting** — setup, compatibility, connectivity, privacy, automations, lighting choices, and real use cases.
3. **Energy, Airflow & Comfort** — drafts, insulation basics, ventilation, condensation, humidity, efficient lighting, and practical comfort improvements.
4. **DIY Projects & Installation** — bounded projects with clear skill level, materials, measurements, steps, finish checks, and safety limits.
5. **Tools, Materials & Buying Decisions** — comparisons and buying guides only where there is a real decision to help readers make; disclose evidence and limitations.

These are proposed editorial pillars. Claude should map current guides into them before changing live navigation. Do not create empty category pages or rename routes without checking inbound links and redirects.

### Explicit exclusions

- Generic decluttering, closet organization, pantry organization, drawer organization, and storage-idea listicles.
- Broad decor inspiration with no practical project or decision to solve.
- Rewritten competitor articles, thin FAQ pages, near-duplicate keyword variants, and pages created only to meet a publishing quota.
- Product “reviews” or “tested” claims without actual testing evidence.
- Unsafe electrical, structural, gas, or plumbing instructions that should be handled by qualified professionals.

## 3. Required competitor audit method

For every site, Claude should inspect at least:
- homepage and primary navigation;
- one relevant category/hub page;
- two recent or representative articles in HomeProjectWise's chosen pillars;
- one buying guide or interactive tool where available;
- mobile rendering for a smaller representative sample.

Record for each reviewed URL:
- date checked and page type;
- intended reader and search intent;
- page title/H1 and visible promise;
- article format (diagnosis, how-to, comparison, cost, checklist, calculator, explainer);
- above-the-fold answer and next step;
- image/diagram usefulness, not just image count;
- tools/materials/time/skill/cost information and whether claims are sourced;
- author, reviewer, methodology, sources, and disclosure signals;
- internal links and related-article logic;
- navigation, readability, accessibility, mobile experience, and intrusive elements;
- one transferable lesson and one thing **not** to copy.

Separate observed facts from hypotheses. Do not call the 20 sites “the top 20” unless a defined, credible ranking source supports that claim.

## 4. Keyword and topic research

Build a keyword-to-intent map rather than a flat list. Use Google Search Console data for the site's own queries/pages; use live search results, autocomplete/related questions, competitor titles and headings, and available keyword tools for discovery. Record source, date, and confidence. Do not invent search volumes or difficulty scores.

Priority intent groups:
- **Troubleshooting:** “why is…”, “how to fix…”, “what causes…”, “how to stop…”
- **Decision/comparison:** “X vs Y”, “which…”, “is X compatible with…”, “what size/type do I need…”
- **Installation/how-to:** “how to install/set up…”, “tools needed…”, “steps to…”
- **Cost and trade-offs:** “cost to…”, “DIY or professional…”, “is it worth…”
- **Prevention and maintenance:** “how often…”, “how to prevent…”, “signs that…”

Illustrative seed topics (validate before assigning):
- how to find and seal a draft around an exterior door;
- bathroom condensation causes and ventilation checks;
- how to choose a dehumidifier capacity for a specific room;
- smart bulb vs smart switch: compatibility and limitations;
- what can safely be plugged into a smart plug;
- smart-home privacy and account-security checklist;
- motion-sensor lighting placement and common setup problems;
- how to diagnose a loose cabinet hinge or wobbly furniture safely;
- energy-saving lighting choices by room/use;
- when a DIY repair should be handed to a professional.

Before approving a new topic, check existing slugs, titles, H1s, meta descriptions, search intent, and internal links. One primary intent should have one clear best page unless there is a documented reason to split.

## 5. Useful-content gate for every new or refreshed guide

A guide is not ready merely because it has a target keyword, a word count, a hero image, or FAQ schema. It must pass all relevant checks:

1. **Audience fit:** clearly belongs to HomeProjectWise and not the organizing sister site.
2. **Direct answer:** answer the main question near the top.
3. **Actionability:** give ordered steps, decision criteria, or a usable diagnostic path.
4. **Original value:** include a meaningful diagram, measurements, decision table, original checklist, worked example, or explanation that improves on available results where appropriate.
5. **Accuracy and safety:** distinguish safe checks from work needing a qualified professional; verify technical claims with primary/authoritative sources.
6. **Evidence and honesty:** link sources; do not claim testing, expertise, savings, prices, or review dates that cannot be substantiated.
7. **Completeness without padding:** cover likely constraints, failure modes, alternatives, and next steps; do not write to a fixed word count.
8. **Visual usefulness:** every image/diagram should match the nearby text, have descriptive alt text, and serve a purpose.
9. **Distinctness:** no near-duplicate of an existing article or another planned page.
10. **Reader journey:** add a small number of genuinely relevant internal links and a clear next step.
11. **Editorial review:** check grammar, headings, broken links, metadata, mobile rendering, and page performance before publication.

If a draft fails a substantive check, revise it or do not publish it. Daily publishing is a cadence target, not permission to publish weak content.

## 6. Site design and architecture principles

Do not redesign by taste alone. First audit the live site and existing CSS/HTML templates. Then prioritize:
1. Make the homepage promise specific: practical home fixes, smart-home help, and improvement projects.
2. Provide clear task-led entry points such as “Fix a problem”, “Install or set up”, and “Reduce energy/comfort issues”, alongside editorial categories if useful.
3. Balance category cards and surface representative guides; avoid empty hubs or one oversized catch-all category.
4. Standardize article components: direct answer, “before you start” details when relevant, steps, safety note, diagram/photo, sources, related guides.
5. Ensure readable contrast, consistent typography/spacing, keyboard focus, accessible navigation, and useful dark-mode contrast.
6. Keep pages lightweight; avoid intrusive popups and layout shifts.
7. Preserve URLs where possible. Any route changes require a redirect/canonical/internal-link plan and validation.
8. Do not copy competitor branding, wording, layout pixel-for-pixel, or proprietary assets.

## 7. Execution sequence for Claude

### Step 1 — Baseline audit (before editing)
- Read this brief, `data/PROJECT_STATE.md`, `data/WORK_LOG.md`, current open PRs, and all relevant files.
- Check live homepage, guide index, category hubs, article template, tools, mobile layout, dark mode, metadata, sitemap, and internal links.
- Report confirmed defects separately from design preferences.
- Do not touch files already changed by open PRs; do not touch workflows or Awin links without explicit authorization.

### Step 2 — Complete the 20-site research
- Use the shortlist above; confirm each site is relevant and active.
- Complete the audit template and add citations/URLs and checked dates.
- Produce a ranked list of no more than 10 transferable improvements, each with impact, effort, risk, and the specific HomeProjectWise page/component it affects.
- Identify content formats and keyword opportunities, but label search volume/difficulty as unknown unless measured with a credible source.

### Step 3 — Build the content map
- Export/collect all current guide titles, slugs, category, primary intent, and target query where known.
- Assign each guide to one pillar and label it keep / improve / re-angle / consider merging.
- Identify gaps and cannibalization risks.
- Do not delete, redirect, or rewrite several pages in one batch without evidence and a rollback plan.

### Step 4 — Implement in small, verifiable changes
- Follow the project's one-small-task-per-PR protocol and add one work-log line per completed task.
- Start with the highest-impact low-risk design or content improvement.
- Keep unrelated CSS, content, workflow, and affiliate changes out of the same PR.
- Run relevant tests/build checks and inspect the deployed result before marking complete.

### Step 5 — Establish a sustainable editorial cadence
- Choose the next guide from validated reader needs and the content map.
- Draft, source-check, review, and publish only after the usefulness gate passes.
- Measure impressions, clicks, queries, indexing, engagement where available, and the actual quality of the page; use those results to refine future topics.

## 8. Success measures

Use baseline and follow-up measurements, not invented targets:
- category distribution and orphan/duplicate-intent pages;
- broken links/images, build status, mobile usability, contrast and performance;
- percentage of guides with useful primary sources and relevant internal links;
- Google Search Console impressions/clicks and query-to-page alignment;
- pages indexed and pages excluded, with reasons;
- whether readers can complete the promised task from the guide.

## 9. Important Google guidance

Google's official people-first content guidance emphasizes original value, trust, clear purpose, accuracy, and satisfying the reader; it explicitly says there is no preferred word count and warns against producing many pages mainly to attract search traffic. Treat that as the editorial standard, not a guarantee of rankings:
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide
