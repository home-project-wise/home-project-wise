# HomeProjectWise: competitor findings, design blueprint, and editorial operating plan

**Research date:** 2026-10-24  
**Scope:** Public-facing pages and current search-visible content patterns across 20 home-improvement, DIY, design, smart-home, and energy-information sites. This is a qualitative product/editorial review, not a traffic ranking or a claim that every page on each site was exhaustively crawled. Search volume, keyword difficulty, revenue, and competitor traffic are not asserted without data.

## Executive decision

HomeProjectWise should not imitate the visual density or publishing scale of large media sites. Its opportunity is to be **calmer, clearer, more practical, more transparent, and more interactive** than a typical general home-advice article.

The differentiator should be: **“Know what to check, what to do next, what it may cost, and when to stop and call a professional.”** Every page should help a reader make a decision or complete a task, not merely consume a list of tips.

Priority sequence:
1. Make the guide library and category routes easy to understand and visually consistent.
2. Standardize a task-first article format with a visible quick answer, safety/stop rules, tools/materials, numbered steps, troubleshooting branches, and next-step links.
3. Build small decision aids from existing expertise (symptom checker, tool/material calculator, cost/time/skill labels), not generic AI gimmicks.
4. Grow topical clusters around repairs, smart home/lighting, energy/comfort, DIY installation, and buying decisions.
5. Publish only when a guide is useful, accurate, distinct, and properly illustrated; one article per day is a ceiling/cadence goal, not a reason to publish weak content.

### Cross-site conclusions

**Patterns to adopt**
- Task-based discovery (fix / install / improve / choose) rather than a flat feed of recent posts.
- Topic hubs that explain what a reader can solve and link to the best next guides.
- Article labels for difficulty, approximate time, tools/materials, and whether a task needs a qualified professional.
- A strong “answer first” opening, numbered steps, relevant images/diagrams, and troubleshooting.
- Clear sources, update dates, author/editor information, correction route, and affiliate disclosure.
- Useful tools/calculators connected directly to relevant guides.
- Visual examples and before/after context when they genuinely clarify the work.
- Different templates for how-to, diagnosis, comparison, and inspiration; not one generic article layout.

**Patterns to avoid**
- Large, noisy homepages filled with unrelated feeds, seasonal promotions, and repeated cards.
- Long intros before the reader gets the answer.
- Generic listicles with no original decision support.
- Fake first-person testing, unsupported savings figures, unverifiable product rankings, or vague “expert reviewed” claims.
- Repeating the same keyword/topic across several articles without a distinct search intent.
- Publishing for a quota when a page lacks evidence, visuals, or a real solution.

## 2. Design direction: a calm, useful workshop

### Visual identity

Use an editorial, modern workshop aesthetic: warm off-white surfaces in light mode, deep ink text, restrained forest/teal accent, muted secondary text, and real task-specific imagery. The site should feel confident and calm, not like a retailer or an ad-heavy content farm.

- Keep the existing brand identity unless a measured audit shows a real issue; refine tokens consistently instead of inventing a new palette in every component.
- Use one display/heading typeface and one highly legible body typeface; keep body copy around 16–18 px on desktop/mobile where the current design allows, with comfortable line height (roughly 1.55–1.75).
- Constrain article text to a comfortable reading width (roughly 65–78 characters per line); wider screens can use a two-column article layout with a narrow contents/utility rail, but mobile must remain a single column.
- Use consistent spacing steps, restrained borders, subtle shadows only where they clarify layers, rounded corners consistently, and no decorative icon overload.
- Every new CSS color must have a tested dark-mode counterpart. Verify text, muted labels, buttons, tables, focus states, diagrams, and affiliate disclosures in both themes.
- Avoid large full-bleed hero areas that push the first useful action below the fold. Use one meaningful image, one promise, and one or two clear actions.

### Homepage information architecture

The homepage should answer within a few seconds: what the site helps with, where to start, and why the advice is trustworthy.

1. **Hero:** concise promise (solve home problems with practical, safer guidance), one primary search/guide-finder route, two clear task paths: “Fix a problem” and “Plan an improvement.” Avoid a vague lifestyle slogan.
2. **Problem picker:** common situations grouped into 4–6 useful entry points, e.g. drafts/moisture, lighting/smart devices, repairs, energy/comfort, tools/material choices. Only link to live, relevant guides.
3. **Featured utility tools:** show 2–4 genuinely useful tools with a one-line explanation; do not crowd the hero with every tool.
4. **Core topic hubs:** visually balanced cards with distinct descriptions and representative tasks. No category should look abandoned or disproportionately dominant.
5. **One editor’s pick / start-here guide:** explain why it is useful and who it helps.
6. **Latest guides:** a compact section, not the whole homepage.
7. **Trust/footer:** editorial standards, how recommendations are evaluated, sources/corrections, about/contact, privacy and commercial disclosures.

### Guide library and category hubs

- Provide a visible search field and filters only if they work well on mobile and are easy to maintain: topic, task type, difficulty, or time.
- Prefer clear, consistent card anatomy: relevant photo/diagram, category label, descriptive title, one-sentence benefit, optional time/difficulty metadata. Align card image ratios and crop.
- Each category hub needs a short introduction, common reader problems, a small set of “start here” guides, related tools, and the full guide list.
- Avoid showing a category label or tool that has no useful content behind it. Keep the number of visible top-level categories manageable.
- Make the empty search/no-results state helpful with alternate terms and category links.

### Article template: hierarchy and comfort

1. Breadcrumbs and topic label.
2. Specific H1 matching the task.
3. A short promise and byline/updated date.
4. A relevant hero image or original diagram with meaningful alt text.
5. **Quick answer / quick win** visible near the top, including the safest first check.
6. **Before you start:** difficulty, realistic time range if supported, tools/materials, likely cost factors, and safety/stop conditions.
7. Table of contents for longer guides, with anchors that work.
8. Numbered steps with one action per step, image/diagram when it adds clarity, and expected result/check after critical steps.
9. Troubleshooting or “if this didn’t work” section.
10. “When to stop and call a professional” for electrical, gas, structural, roof-height, refrigerant, mold/asbestos/lead, or other hazardous work as applicable.
11. Sources and further reading; related guides chosen by reader task rather than keyword alone.
12. Author/editor context, last updated, correction/contact path, and a clear disclosure where commercial links exist.
13. A compact next-step CTA and related guides; avoid popups, sticky overlays, and intrusive interruptions.

### Mobile, performance, and accessibility

- Prioritize the first screen: headline, immediate answer, and primary action should not be buried.
- Use touch targets around 44×44 CSS px where practical, clear keyboard focus, semantic headings, descriptive links, and accessible form labels.
- Images need fixed dimensions/aspect ratio to reduce layout shift, responsive sizes, compressed files, and descriptive alt text. Do not force 8–12 images into every article; use only images that teach something.
- Keep scripts light; prefer static HTML and small progressive-enhancement interactions consistent with the Hugo site. No new heavy JS framework for a small widget.
- Check real mobile widths, light/dark mode, 200% zoom, keyboard navigation, contrast, broken images/links, and performance before approving visual changes.
- Avoid ad placements that interrupt instructions. The article should remain easy to use with one hand on a phone.

## 3. Original features that can differentiate the site

These are proposed experiments, not features claimed to exist already. Build one at a time and measure use.

1. **“Start with the symptom” diagnosis paths:** choose symptom → answer 2–5 safe questions → receive likely checks ranked by safety/ease, plus a stop/professional boundary. Never diagnose hazards with false certainty.
2. **Every guide ends with “What to do next”:** if fixed, prevention; if not fixed, next diagnostic guide; if unsafe/complex, stop and seek qualified help.
3. **One-page job card:** printable checklist of tools, materials, steps, measurements, and final checks generated from the guide content.
4. **Honest effort labels:** beginner/intermediate/advanced, time range, number of tools, mess/noise, and whether power/water must be shut off—only where editorially verified.
5. **Decision tables instead of generic “best” lists:** compare options by room constraints, compatibility, repairability, running cost, installation difficulty, privacy, and trade-offs.
6. **“Before you buy” compatibility checker** for smart-home articles: ecosystem, Wi-Fi band/protocol, hub needs, subscription, power source, privacy/support caveats. Only add checks the site can keep accurate.
7. **Maintenance calendar by home system:** simple monthly/seasonal checklist with links to evidence-based guides; climate- and region-specific notes clearly marked.
8. **Cost and energy calculators with visible assumptions:** show formulas, units, ranges and caveats; do not promise savings without evidence.
9. **Photo/diagram standard:** each visual must answer a question—where the part is, what correct/incorrect looks like, what changes between states, or how to measure. No decorative stock-photo quota.
10. **Repair confidence note:** “safe first checks / DIY boundary / call a pro if…” on relevant pages, reviewed for risk.

## 4. Editorial plan: how to choose topics and build clusters

### Editorial pillars

1. Home Repairs & Troubleshooting
2. Smart Home & Lighting
3. Energy, Airflow & Comfort
4. DIY Projects & Installation
5. Tools, Materials & Buying Decisions

Do not drift into general decluttering, closet/drawer/pantry organization, or routine tidying; those belong to Home Organization Ideas. A room is in scope only when the intent is repair, installation, energy/comfort, safety, a technical choice, or a defined DIY improvement.

### Topic selection scorecard

Score each candidate from 0–2 on: (a) clear user problem/search intent, (b) fit to one pillar, (c) evidence and source availability, (d) ability to offer original diagrams/checklists/decision support, (e) non-duplication with existing site content, (f) realistic maintenance/accuracy burden. Reject any candidate that fails safety/evidence or duplicates an existing URL regardless of total score.

Before writing:
- Search the exact problem phrase and close variants.
- Review search results and relevant competitor pages; record the intent, the subquestions they answer, and the important questions they miss.
- Use Search Console data when available; use a keyword tool only when its source and date are recorded. Do not invent search volume, difficulty, RPM, or competitor traffic.
- Inspect the current site’s article inventory and internal links before creating a new URL.
- Choose one primary intent and a small set of genuinely related secondary questions. Do not target multiple unrelated intents in one page.
- Write the reader’s task in one sentence: “After this guide, the reader can ___ safely and confidently.”

### Four article formats (do not force one template onto every intent)

**A. Troubleshooting guide**
- Symptom and likely causes, ordered from simple/low-risk to complex.
- Safe checks first; what each result means; when to stop.
- Fixes by cause, verification after the fix, prevention, and professional boundary.
- Sources for technical, safety, energy, or code-related claims.

**B. How-to / installation guide**
- Outcome and suitability; skill/time/tools/materials; preparation and safety.
- Numbered steps with action + expected result + common mistake.
- Measurements/compatibility, finish checklist, maintenance, and next steps.
- Original step photos or diagrams wherever text alone could cause a mistake.

**C. Comparison / buying decision**
- Who each option is for, must-have criteria, compatibility/constraints, installation and ownership costs, disadvantages, support/privacy/repairability.
- State evidence level and methodology; distinguish hands-on tests from research.
- Include “choose X if / avoid X if” and a transparent conclusion; disclose affiliate relationships.
- Do not make unsupported universal “best” claims.

**D. Explainer / planning guide**
- Direct answer, how the system works, what changes the decision, common misconceptions, costs/benefits with sourced assumptions, and practical next steps.
- Use diagrams and examples; avoid padded history or generic introductions.

### Writing standard for each guide

- Lead with the answer or the safest useful action in the first 80–120 words; do not begin with a long generic introduction.
- Use descriptive H2s framed around reader questions or task stages. No artificial quota for number of headings or words.
- Prefer short paragraphs, active voice, precise verbs, tables only when they aid a decision, and numbered lists for procedures.
- Be specific: dimensions, units, compatibility, expected outcomes, limits, and examples only when verified.
- Add original value competitors cannot get by paraphrasing one another: a decision tree, annotated diagram, checklist, measured comparison, clear failure mode, or tested procedure. Never fabricate hands-on experience.
- Cite primary/authoritative sources for safety, electrical, building science, energy, technical specifications, and claims that could materially affect a decision. Use relevant manufacturer documentation for model-specific setup, but cross-check where possible.
- Explain uncertainty and regional variation. Avoid applying US codes, rebates, voltage assumptions, or product availability globally.
- FAQ sections should answer real unresolved questions, not repeat the body or exist solely to target keywords.
- Internal links should help the reader’s next step: prerequisite, related symptom, alternative solution, maintenance, tool, or comparison. Avoid forced links.
- Review images for subject match, technical accuracy, accessibility, licensing/ownership, file size, and mobile crop.
- Final editorial check: could a reader safely act on this? Is every claim supported? Is it distinct from our own pages? Is the next step clear? If not, revise or do not publish.

### Article outline template

**Title:** [specific problem/task] + [constraint or outcome when helpful]  
**Opening:** direct answer + who this applies to + key safety/compatibility caveat.  
**Quick answer:** 2–5 actionable bullets.  
**Before you start:** skill, time, tools/materials, constraints.  
**Steps / causes / options:** tailored to the article format.  
**Check your result:** observable pass/fail signs.  
**If it still fails:** next safe check or a related guide.  
**When to call a professional:** risk-specific boundary.  
**Maintenance / prevention:** only relevant items.  
**Sources:** primary sources first.  
**Related next steps:** 2–4 highly relevant internal links.  
**FAQ:** only genuine remaining questions.

Word count is determined by the task. A simple troubleshooting answer may be concise; a multi-stage installation or comparison needs more detail. Do not aim at 1,500–2,500 words just to satisfy a template.

## 5. Publishing plan: steady, clustered, quality-gated

### First 30 days

**Days 1–3: inventory and baseline**
- Export every live guide: URL, title, pillar, format, intent, update date, image status, sources, internal links, and known Search Console/Bing performance if accessible.
- Mark duplicates/cannibalization candidates; do not delete or redirect until evidence and a replacement plan exist.
- Audit the live homepage, guide index, category hubs, one tool page, and at least three representative articles on mobile/light/dark.
- Recheck current image-audit and traffic-report status; report unavailable data as unavailable, never estimate it.

**Days 4–7: fix navigation and one article template issue at a time**
- Choose the highest-impact confirmed issue, not the most visually exciting idea.
- Check open PRs and touched files before each change; keep each PR small; run repository checks.
- Ensure category cards, article metadata, image ratios, and dark-mode contrast are consistent.

**Week 2: cluster and improve**
- Select one pillar with an existing base of guides and build a hub + internal-link map.
- Improve 2–3 existing guides where there is a clear gap in accuracy, clarity, original diagrams, safety boundaries, or internal links.
- Add no new guide if an existing URL can satisfy the intent with a meaningful update.

**Week 3: add one distinctive utility**
- Select one small tool (diagnosis path, checklist, or compatibility aid) linked from 2–3 relevant guides.
- State assumptions and test normal, edge, and invalid inputs; keep it accessible and mobile-first.

**Week 4: publish a connected mini-cluster**
- Publish only the missing guides in one coherent cluster. A sustainable target is up to one quality article per day, but slower is preferable to repetitive or unsupported content.
- Each new guide should link to its hub, one prerequisite/related guide, and one useful next step.
- Review Search Console/Bing impressions and clicks after sufficient time; do not judge indexing or rankings from a one-day snapshot.

### Weekly rhythm after the first month

- Monday: search intent, source gathering, overlap check, outline.
- Tuesday: draft and verify claims.
- Wednesday: diagrams/images, edit, accessibility and safety review.
- Thursday: publish if the quality gate passes; otherwise revise.
- Friday: internal links, technical checks, distribution, and performance review.
- Daily publishing may happen when the queue is ready; it must not override this gate.

### Launch gate (all must pass)

- One clear intent and no unresolved overlap with an existing article.
- Direct answer near the top; no padded intro.
- Claims verified; high-risk instructions have appropriate boundaries and sources.
- Original value (diagram, checklist, decision table, useful procedure, or clearly useful synthesis).
- Relevant visuals with correct alt text and no misleading image.
- Useful internal links and correct canonical/metadata/structured data where applicable.
- Mobile, dark-mode, link/image, and build checks pass.
- Affiliate disclosures and product claims are accurate.
- Editorial update date is truthful; do not change it merely to look fresh.

## 6. Search and measurement discipline

Track the following by page and pillar: impressions, clicks, CTR, average position (as a directional metric), index status, referral traffic, engaged sessions if available, tool interactions, and internal-link clicks. Separate measured data from interpretation.

For keyword research, maintain a small evidence table: query, source, capture date, observed intent/SERP features, current site URL (if any), competitor examples, content gap, confidence, and next action. If a tool cannot supply reliable volume/difficulty, leave the field blank instead of guessing.

Do not equate “indexed” with “successful.” First confirm a URL is valid, canonical, internally linked, in the sitemap, useful, and eligible; then monitor discovery and performance over time.

## 7. Implementation order and boundaries

1. Complete a live baseline audit before any broad redesign.
2. Convert these recommendations into a prioritized backlog with impact, effort, evidence, risk, and file ownership.
3. Avoid editing any file touched by another open PR; do not touch Awin links, `.editorial-freeze`, or workflows without explicit authorization.
4. Preserve the current site architecture unless the audit demonstrates a concrete problem.
5. Every visual change needs a dark-mode counterpart and contrast verification.
6. Make one small, testable change per PR, with one work-log entry in the same PR. Run the relevant checks and report exact outcomes.
7. Do not call the full 20-site review exhaustive; repeat the review for specific page types before major implementation.
8. Keep HomeProjectWise focused on repair, smart home, energy/comfort, installation, and buying decisions—not general decluttering/organization.

## What this document does and does not claim

This document records a qualitative review of public-facing patterns across the 20 listed reference sites and converts those patterns into an actionable design/editorial blueprint. It does **not** claim that a full crawl of every site, quantitative keyword research, competitor traffic analysis, user testing, or an audit of every live HomeProjectWise page has been completed. Those require separate evidence gathering and must be reported honestly.
